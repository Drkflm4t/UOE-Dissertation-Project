Summary: This paper proposes a pretraining-and-transfer framework for EEG-to-text decoding. The core contribution is CET-MAE, a multimodal self-supervised model that combines masked reconstruction of EEG and text with EEG-text contrastive alignment via a multi-stream encoder. The downstream framework, E2T-PTR, transfers the pretrained EEG-side modules into a BART-based decoder for open-vocabulary text generation from EEG. Experiments on ZuCo reading-task combinations (3, 4, and 5 tasks) show improvements over prior EEG-to-text baselines on BLEU and ROUGE, with ablations suggesting benefits from sentence-level EEG features, the combined SSL objectives, and the multi-stream design.

Strengths: Timely and relevant problem: open-vocabulary EEG-to-text decoding is important and challenging, and the paper targets a meaningful gap in multimodal EEG-text pretraining.; Clear technical idea: combining cross-modal contrastive learning with intra-modal masked reconstruction is well motivated, and the multi-stream architecture is conceptually sensible.; Good empirical coverage on the target benchmark: results are reported on multiple ZuCo task combinations (3/4/5 tasks), with comparisons to EEG2Text, DeWave, and C-SCL.; Useful ablations: the paper studies sentence-level EEG inclusion, CET vs ET-MAE vs CET-MAE, masking ratios, masking strategy, and multi-stream design.; The paper identifies a practical issue in ZuCo—missing eye-fixation-aligned word EEG—and proposes concatenating sentence-level EEG as a mitigation, which is a thoughtful dataset-aware design choice.; Qualitative examples and appendix analyses provide some interpretability into what the model is learning and how performance changes with more training data.

Weaknesses: Novelty is somewhat incremental relative to existing multimodal SSL literature: the main idea is an adaptation of contrastive + masked autoencoding to EEG-text rather than a fundamentally new learning principle.; Evaluation is confined to ZuCo, a text-evoked reading dataset; claims about broader applicability to inner speech BCIs are speculative and not experimentally validated.; The reported gains over some baselines are modest in several settings, especially against reproduced C-SCL/EEG2Text variants, and significance testing is absent.; The leave-one-subject-out results are unexpectedly better than the standard split results, which is counterintuitive and suggests possible differences in split difficulty or leakage-like confounds that are not sufficiently analyzed.; The paper acknowledges reliance on teacher forcing, which limits the realism of the decoding setup and weakens claims about practical open-ended generation.; Methodological details around preprocessing, alignment, and exact use of sentence-level EEG in pretraining/fine-tuning could be clearer; some design choices appear heuristic (e.g., forced masking of sentence-level EEG, chosen loss weights, 75% masking via trial-and-error).; Comparisons may not be fully apples-to-apples because training sample counts differ across methods/settings in Table 1, and some baselines are reproduced while others are taken from prior work.

Soundness Issues: The overall approach is plausible and technically coherent, but the empirical validation does not fully rule out alternative explanations for gains, especially given dataset-specific artifacts and limited benchmark diversity.; The use of sentence-level EEG concatenated with word-level EEG may inject strong global information that changes the task formulation relative to prior methods; this is a reasonable design, but fairness and contribution attribution should be discussed more carefully.; No statistical significance or variance across runs is reported, despite relatively small absolute improvements in several metrics.; The stronger leave-one-subject-out performance than random split performance is surprising; without a careful explanation of split construction and sample overlap properties, this raises concerns about evaluation comparability.; The paper uses text-evoked EEG recorded during reading, so decoding may partly exploit correlations with perceived text rather than demonstrating general language-from-brain decoding; the paper should be more careful in framing implications for imagined/inner speech.; Qualitative outputs still contain substantial hallucination and corruption, indicating that practical decoding quality remains limited despite metric gains.
