Summary: This paper proposes CET-MAE, a multimodal self-supervised pretraining framework for EEG-text representation learning that combines masked reconstruction of EEG/text with EEG-text contrastive alignment via a multi-stream encoder, and E2T-PTR, a downstream EEG-to-text decoding framework that transfers the pretrained EEG-side representations into a BART-based generator. Experiments on ZuCo reading-task combinations show consistent improvements over prior EEG-to-text baselines on BLEU and ROUGE metrics, with ablations suggesting benefits from sentence-level EEG features, the combined SSL objective, forced masking of sentence-level EEG, and the multi-stream design.

Strengths: Addresses an important and timely problem: open-vocabulary EEG-to-text decoding for BCI applications.; Technically well-motivated combination of cross-modal contrastive learning and intra-modal masked reconstruction; the hybrid SSL design is sensible for EEG-text alignment.; The proposed CET-MAE pretraining objective is clearly structured and the downstream transfer setup (E2T-PTR) is intuitive.; Empirical results on ZuCo show consistent gains over strong prior baselines such as EEG2Text and DeWave in the 3-task and 5-task settings.; Ablation studies are fairly comprehensive: sentence-level EEG inclusion, CET-MAE vs no pretraining, E2T-PTR transfer, masking ratios, contrastive-only vs masking-only vs combined SSL, masking strategy, and multi-stream design.; The paper identifies a practical issue in ZuCo—missing eye-fixation-aligned word EEG—and proposes concatenating sentence-level EEG as a mitigation, which is a useful dataset-aware design choice.; Qualitative examples and appendix analyses provide additional insight into model behavior and subject variability.

Weaknesses: The empirical gains, while consistent, are modest in several settings, especially relative to reproduced C-SCL results in the 4-task setup where improvements are mixed and ROUGE-1 F1 is slightly worse.; The evaluation remains confined to ZuCo; claims about broader transferability and applicability to inner-speech BCIs are speculative without testing on additional datasets or paradigms.; The paper acknowledges continued reliance on teacher forcing / implicit ground-truth conditioning assumptions common in prior work, which limits the practical significance of the decoding setup.; Methodological details are sometimes underspecified or potentially confusing, e.g., exact use of BART/BERT terminology in masking and encoding, and how decoding is performed at test time.; The leave-one-subject-out results appearing better than the standard split are surprising and insufficiently explained; this raises questions about split difficulty, leakage, or comparability of evaluation protocols.; Some qualitative generations remain far from fluent or faithful under 3-task and 4-task settings, indicating that absolute decoding quality is still limited despite metric improvements.; The paper occasionally overstates novelty/superiority (e.g., 'first' claims, 'significantly' better) without always fully substantiating them against all relevant prior multimodal EEG-language work.

Soundness Issues: Potential evaluation ambiguity around 'teacher forcing' and the statement that the model operates with an 'implicit understanding' of ground-truth tokens during testing; this should be clarified precisely to rule out label leakage or overly assisted decoding.; The comparison across methods may not be fully apples-to-apples because training sample counts differ across baselines/settings, and some results are reproduced while others are taken from prior papers.; Hyperparameter choices such as the 75% masking ratio are justified mainly by trial-and-error rather than a principled search; this is acceptable but weakens methodological rigor.; The reported improvement in leave-one-subject-out validation over the standard split is counterintuitive and deserves stronger analysis to establish that the protocol is sound and comparable.; No statistical significance testing or variance over runs is reported, which is important given the relatively small datasets and modest metric differences.; The paper does not deeply analyze whether gains come from better EEG-text alignment versus simply stronger language-model priors from BART fine-tuning.
