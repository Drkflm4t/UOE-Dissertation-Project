Summary: The paper proposes Masked thought Fine-Tuning (MFT), a simple supervised fine-tuning variant for mathematical reasoning in which random tokens in the chain-of-thought/rationale are masked or replaced during training while the original target sequence remains unchanged. The authors argue this acts as a regularizer that reduces over-reliance on short-range context and encourages stronger dependence on the question and earlier reasoning steps. Experiments on GSM8K, MATH, GSM-IC, several augmented math datasets, and two base model families (Llama-2 and Mistral-7B) show consistent gains over standard SFT, especially on smaller or simpler reasoning datasets, and complementary gains when combined with augmentation methods such as RFT, MetaMathQA, and MathInstruct. The paper also includes analyses comparing different noise types, dependency statistics, distractor robustness, and limited error analysis to support the proposed mechanism.

Strengths: Very simple and low-overhead method: the core idea is easy to implement and integrates directly into standard SFT pipelines.; Empirical gains are fairly consistent across multiple training datasets and two model families, with especially clear improvements on GSM8K and GSM-IC.; Good practical positioning: the method does not require external annotators, larger teacher models, or expensive self-sampling to obtain benefits.; Useful ablations on noise type and placement show that masking/replacing partial reasoning tokens is more effective than several alternative regularization strategies considered.; The paper goes beyond headline accuracy and investigates robustness to distractors (GSM-IC), sample efficiency, and compatibility with existing augmentation pipelines.; The dependency-based analysis and case studies provide an intuitively plausible explanation for why the method may help reasoning.; The paper is generally well organized, with appendices covering implementation details, additional cases, and comparisons.

Weaknesses: The central mechanistic claim about improving long-distance dependency learning is suggestive but not conclusively established; the evidence is correlational rather than causal.; The evaluation is concentrated on relatively small open models and mostly math reasoning benchmarks; it is unclear how well the method scales to stronger contemporary models or broader reasoning settings.; Some comparisons are not perfectly controlled because MFT is often trained for more epochs than SFT, which complicates attribution of gains purely to masking.; The method relies on chain-of-thought style supervised traces, so its applicability may be limited when such rationales are unavailable, undesirable, or suppressed.; OOD generalization results are mixed, including slight degradation on some MathInstruct/MetaMathQA settings, which weakens claims of broad transfer.; Several analyses depend on GPT-4-based judging or custom dependency probes whose reliability and sensitivity are not deeply validated.; The paper does not fully explore trade-offs with general instruction-following quality, mixed-data training, or inference-time behavior beyond final-answer accuracy.

Soundness Issues: The training-budget mismatch is a notable methodological concern: SFT commonly uses 3 epochs while MFT uses 5-10 epochs depending on dataset size. Although the authors argue SFT does not benefit similarly from longer training, the main tables are still not strictly compute-matched.; The dependency probe perturbs numeric tokens and measures downstream changes, but this proxy may not cleanly isolate semantic dependency versus superficial sensitivity; stronger validation of the probe would improve confidence.; The claim that MFT is comparable to RFT should be interpreted carefully because RFT and MFT differ in both data volume and supervision source; the comparison is practically interesting but not a like-for-like methodological equivalence.; False-positive and error-type analyses rely on GPT-4 judgments over relatively small samples, which introduces evaluator noise and limited statistical power.; Hyperparameter choices such as mask ratio and long warm-up appear important, but tuning effort may vary by dataset; the paper does not fully quantify sensitivity or robustness across settings.
