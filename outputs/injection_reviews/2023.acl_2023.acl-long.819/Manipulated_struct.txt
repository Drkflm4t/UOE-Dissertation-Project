Summary: The paper studies false negatives in cross-modal contrastive learning for vision-language pre-training. It revisits InfoNCE from a mutual-information perspective and argues that, when false negatives are non-negligible, optimizing InfoNCE effectively maximizes a lower bound on MI(anchor, positive) minus expected MI(anchor, negatives), which can improperly suppress semantically related negatives. To address this, the authors propose Similarity-Regulated Contrastive Learning (SRCL), which assigns each negative a weight inversely proportional to estimated cross-modal similarity, with similarity progressively refined using a frozen auxiliary model trained on human-annotated data and the evolving VLP model itself. Implemented on top of ALBEF, SRCL improves over the ALBEF baseline on image-text retrieval, zero-shot retrieval, VQA, and NLVR, with qualitative analyses suggesting a more semantically structured representation space.

Strengths: Targets an important and practically relevant issue in vision-language contrastive learning: partial/false negatives arising from many-to-many image-text correspondences.; Provides a clear conceptual motivation and a reasonably coherent MI-based analysis connecting false negatives to the behavior of InfoNCE.; The proposed method is simple to integrate into an existing VLP framework: it mainly modifies contrastive weighting rather than requiring a new architecture.; Empirical results are consistent across several downstream tasks, with improvements over the ALBEF baseline on retrieval, zero-shot retrieval, VQA, and NLVR.; The zero-shot retrieval gains are notable and align well with the paper's claim that SRCL improves semantic structure in the embedding space.; Includes ablation-style analysis on the tradeoff between removing likely false negatives and preserving hard negatives, which helps motivate why weighting may be preferable to outright filtering.; Qualitative examples and contrastive-weight visualizations support the intuition that semantically related negatives receive smaller penalties.

Weaknesses: The method is evaluated primarily by plugging into ALBEF; broader validation on other modern VLP backbones would strengthen claims of generality.; The theoretical development relies on several approximations and assumptions that are not fully justified empirically, especially the use of cross-modal similarity as a proxy for mutual information.; The auxiliary similarity estimator Hβ is trained on human-annotated data, which adds extra supervision and complexity; the contribution of this extra model versus the weighting mechanism itself is not fully disentangled.; The paper does not provide sufficiently detailed ablations on key design choices such as the schedule for alpha, the scaling factor delta, normalization strategy, and the dependence on the auxiliary model quality.; Reported gains over ALBEF, while consistent, are moderate on several tasks, so the practical significance beyond a strong baseline could be better contextualized.; Some presentation and notation are occasionally confusing or inconsistent, especially in the theoretical appendix and variable naming around similarities and weights.; There is limited discussion of computational overhead introduced by training and using the auxiliary model for similarity estimation.

Soundness Issues: The MI derivation appears suggestive rather than fully rigorous. Several steps depend on approximations over batch expectations and assumptions about the proportion and behavior of false negatives.; A central assumption is that cross-modal similarity positively correlates with MI and can serve as its proxy; this is plausible but not validated directly.; The proof for controllable positive MI over negatives depends on conditions about covariance and normalized expected weights, but the practical implementation may only approximately satisfy these conditions.; Because Hβ is trained on curated human-annotated data, improvements may partly stem from injecting a better similarity prior rather than solely from correcting InfoNCE's treatment of false negatives.; The empirical evidence supports effectiveness on the tested setup, but the paper does not fully isolate whether gains come from theory-driven weighting, auxiliary supervision, or implementation details inherited from ALBEF.
