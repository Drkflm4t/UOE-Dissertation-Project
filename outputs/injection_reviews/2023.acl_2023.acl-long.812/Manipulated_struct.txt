Summary: This paper proposes Grouped Head Attention (GHA) to reduce redundancy in multi-head attention by explicitly grouping heads so that heads within a group become similar while different groups become distinct. The method uses a self-supervised Group-Constrained Training (GCT) objective based on clustering-derived hidden units from attention-related feature maps, and then applies a Voting-to-Stay (V2S) pruning procedure to retain one representative 'Pillar of Strength' head per group, yielding a compressed variant (GHA-PS / GHT-PS). Experiments on machine translation, abstractive summarization, and language modeling report consistent gains over vanilla Transformers and several prior efficiency-oriented baselines, along with substantial parameter reductions and some inference/FLOP improvements.

Strengths: The paper addresses an important and well-motivated problem: redundancy and over-parameterization in multi-head attention.; The proposed approach is conceptually interesting: it combines intra-group homogenization with inter-group diversification, then exploits the induced structure for pruning.; The method is evaluated on three distinct NLP tasks (MT, summarization, LM), which strengthens the empirical case for generality.; Results in the tables on pages 6 and 8 show improvements not only over the vanilla Transformer backbone but also over several prior head-reduction or efficient-attention baselines.; The paper includes useful ablations (page 6) that test the roles of diversification, homogenization, GCT, HUDS, and the PS selection mechanism.; The analysis sections are stronger than average: the plots on page 7 examine group compactness, number of groups, and training dynamics of intra-/inter-group behavior, helping support the paper's intuition.; The compressed GHT-PS variant appears practically relevant, with notable parameter savings and competitive or improved performance.; The presentation of the method is fairly clear overall, and the figures/tables help communicate the intended grouping-and-pruning pipeline.

Weaknesses: The method introduces several design choices and hyperparameters (e.g., clustering choice, number of groups, alpha/beta, feature map selection V/A/O), and the final configuration appears dataset-specific, which may limit robustness and ease of adoption.; The grouping supervision depends on an unsupervised clustering module (HUDS/K-means), but the stability and sensitivity of this clustering process are not deeply analyzed.; Comparisons are mostly against older or selective baselines; the paper does not benchmark against more recent efficient Transformer variants or broader pruning/compression methods beyond MHA-focused ones.; Some claimed gains are modest on certain datasets, and it is not always fully clear whether improvements stem from the grouping principle itself versus additional regularization/tuning.; The inference-speed improvements are inconsistent in some tables and sometimes small relative to parameter reduction, suggesting practical efficiency gains may depend on implementation details.; The method is only validated on NLP tasks and models trained from scratch; applicability to pretrained large language models or other modalities remains untested.; The paper could better clarify computational overhead during training from repeated clustering/group supervision, since training-time efficiency is less emphasized than inference-time efficiency.

Soundness Issues: The core hypothesis is plausible and supported empirically, but the causal claim that increased group compactness directly improves performance is only correlational in the analysis on page 7, not conclusively established.; The use of clustering-derived hidden units as self-supervision may create a moving-target optimization problem; the paper does not fully analyze convergence behavior, variance across runs, or failure cases.; The fairness of some comparisons may be debatable because the proposed method uses dataset-specific tuning of grouping coefficients and feature-map choices (appendix tables on page 13), while it is unclear whether competing baselines received comparable tuning effort.; The V2S pruning rule retains one head per group based on voting over feature-map-derived scores, but there is limited theoretical justification that this criterion best preserves task utility beyond empirical evidence.; Statistical significance is reported in several tables, which is good, but the paper does not provide much detail on number of runs/variance for all main results, making robustness somewhat hard to assess.
