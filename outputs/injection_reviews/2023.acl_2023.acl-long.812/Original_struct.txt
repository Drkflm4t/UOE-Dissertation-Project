Summary: The paper proposes Grouped Head Attention (GHA) to reduce redundancy in multi-head attention by explicitly grouping heads during training. A self-supervised Group-Constrained Training (GCT) objective encourages heads within a group to become similar while pushing different groups apart, using hidden group assignments discovered by clustering head feature maps (value projections, attention maps, or head outputs). After convergence, a Voting-to-Stay (V2S) pruning procedure retains one representative 'Pillar of Strength' head per group and masks the others, yielding a compressed variant (GHA-PS / GHT-PS). Experiments on machine translation, abstractive summarization, and language modeling report improved task performance over vanilla Transformers and several prior efficiency baselines, while reducing parameters, FLOPs, and inference cost. The paper also includes ablations on the grouping objective, group compactness, and number of groups.

Strengths: The paper addresses an important and well-motivated problem: redundancy and over-parameterization in multi-head attention.; The proposed approach is conceptually intuitive: combine intra-group homogenization with inter-group diversification, then prune within-group redundant heads.; The method is evaluated on three distinct NLP tasks (machine translation, summarization, language modeling), which improves breadth of empirical validation.; Results are generally strong relative to the chosen baselines, with consistent gains reported in several settings and meaningful parameter reductions for the pruned variant.; The paper includes useful ablations, including removing diversification/homogenization terms, disabling GCT before pruning, random head retention, and varying the number of groups.; The efficiency discussion includes parameters, FLOPs, and inference speed, not just accuracy metrics.; The visual and analytical discussion of group compactness and training dynamics provides some interpretability for the proposed mechanism.

Weaknesses: The technical formulation is underspecified and at points inconsistent, making it difficult to verify correctness or reproduce the method precisely.; The hidden-unit discovery mechanism relies on clustering intermediate head features during training, but the paper does not clearly specify when and how often clustering is run, whether assignments are stable, differentiable, or computationally expensive.; The method introduces many dataset-specific hyperparameter choices (alpha, beta, feature map selection, group count), and the final appendix shows substantial per-dataset tuning, raising concerns about robustness and fairness of comparison.; Comparisons are limited to relatively old Transformer baselines and efficiency methods; there is no evaluation on modern pretrained Transformer settings or stronger contemporary architectures.; The claimed statistical significance is not very convincing because the paper does not clearly report the number of runs, variance, or exact testing protocol for all experiments.; The pruning procedure appears to require a separate voting epoch after convergence and then finetuning, which adds training complexity; this overhead is not quantified.; The paper's central claim that compact grouping identifies the 'most representative and distinctive features' is plausible but not rigorously established beyond correlation plots and task performance.; The method is only validated in NLP and on models trained from scratch, limiting the practical significance of the conclusions.

Methodological Flaws: Equation 4 is not presented with sufficient mathematical clarity. The notation for hidden units, group-conditioned feature maps, and probabilities is ambiguous, and some symbols are overloaded or inconsistently indexed. This makes it hard to determine whether the objective is well-defined as written.; The scientific logic around 'minimum-redundancy feature selection' is largely heuristic. The paper does not provide a formal argument that the proposed grouping objective minimizes redundancy in any principled information-theoretic or optimization sense.; The use of K-means-based hidden unit discovery inside training is problematic from an optimization standpoint: clustering assignments are non-differentiable and potentially unstable, yet the paper does not analyze convergence behavior, assignment noise, or sensitivity to initialization.; The claim that higher group compactness improves performance is supported only by empirical correlation on development runs (Figure 2), which does not establish causality. Moreover, the paper itself shows performance eventually degrades under stronger compactness, weakening the generality of the claim.; The V2S pruning rule is heuristic. Selecting one head per group by vote count over batches lacks theoretical justification, and there is no comparison against simpler alternatives such as magnitude pruning, importance scores, or selecting cluster centroids directly under the same training setup.; Empirical fairness is questionable because the proposed method uses dataset-specific choices of feature map type and regularization strengths (appendix tables), while it is unclear whether competing baselines received comparable tuning effort.; Inference-speed comparisons may be incomplete because the paper reports real head removal only at test time, but training-time overhead from HUDS, clustering, voting, and finetuning is omitted. Thus the overall efficiency claim is only partial.; The paper does not report variance/error bars in the main tables, despite making broad claims of consistent gains. Given that some improvements are modest on certain datasets, lack of multi-seed robustness weakens empirical validity.; The pruning compression claims can be misleading because parameter counts exclude embeddings, and some comparisons use specially downsized baselines rather than standard matched-training-budget baselines. This may overstate practical compression benefits.; There is no analysis of whether gains come from the grouping regularizer itself versus simply adding an auxiliary loss or structured pruning schedule. A stronger control would be needed to isolate the true source of improvement.
