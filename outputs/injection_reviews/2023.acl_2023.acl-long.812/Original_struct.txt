Summary: This paper proposes Grouped Head Attention (GHA) to reduce redundancy in multi-head attention by explicitly grouping heads during training. The method uses a self-supervised Group-Constrained Training (GCT) objective that encourages homogenization within groups and diversification across groups, using hidden group assignments discovered from attention-related feature maps via unsupervised clustering. A subsequent Voting-to-Stay (V2S) pruning procedure retains one representative 'Pillar of Strength' head per group and removes the others, yielding a lighter model (GHT-PS). Experiments on machine translation, abstractive summarization, and language modeling show consistent gains over vanilla Transformers and several prior efficiency-oriented baselines, while reducing parameters, FLOPs, and sometimes improving inference speed.

Strengths: The paper addresses an important and well-motivated problem: redundancy and over-parameterization in multi-head attention.; The proposed approach is conceptually clear and nicely combines two complementary ideas: structured grouping during training and post hoc pruning of redundant heads.; The method is evaluated on three distinct NLP tasks (machine translation, summarization, language modeling), which strengthens the empirical case for generality.; Results are strong against both vanilla Transformer baselines and several prior methods for head redundancy reduction or efficient attention.; The ablation study is reasonably thorough, isolating the effects of homogenization, diversification, GCT, HUDS, and the PS selection mechanism.; The analysis sections go beyond headline metrics: the paper studies group compactness, number of groups, training dynamics, and efficiency trade-offs.; The pruning procedure is simple and practical, and the reported parameter/FLOP reductions are substantial while preserving or improving accuracy.

Weaknesses: The core grouping mechanism relies on unsupervised clustering of head feature maps, but the paper gives limited justification for why K-means over these chosen features should reliably recover meaningful head groups.; The method introduces several design choices and hyperparameters (choice of feature map, alpha/beta weights, number of groups, clustering setup), and optimal settings appear dataset-specific, which may limit robustness and ease of adoption.; The theoretical grounding is suggestive rather than rigorous; claims about representative/distinctive feature subsets and reduced redundancy are mostly supported empirically rather than formally established.; Comparisons are mostly against older efficient-attention or pruning baselines; the paper does not test against a broader set of stronger contemporary compression/efficient Transformer methods.; The pruning criterion in V2S is somewhat heuristic, and there is limited analysis of its stability across runs or sensitivity to the voting epoch and convergence threshold.; The paper focuses on training from scratch and standard NLP benchmarks; applicability to pretrained large language models or non-NLP domains is left untested.; Some efficiency comparisons are not perfectly apples-to-apples because different baselines may differ in implementation maturity or architecture beyond attention alone.

Soundness Issues: The methodology is generally sensible and empirically validated, but some causal claims are stronger than what the evidence strictly supports. In particular, improved performance is attributed to learning the 'most representative and distinctive' features, yet this is inferred indirectly from clustering compactness metrics rather than directly demonstrated.; The hidden-unit discovery and grouping process depends on clustering intermediate representations during training, but the paper does not deeply analyze whether group assignments are stable, reproducible, or semantically meaningful across seeds/layers.; Hyperparameter tuning appears important and dataset-specific (as shown in the appendix tables for alpha/beta/feature-map choices), raising concern that gains may partly depend on substantial task-specific tuning effort.; Statistical significance is reported for many results, which is good, but the paper provides limited detail in the main text about the number of runs/seeds for all experiments, making it harder to assess robustness.; Despite these concerns, the experimental design, ablations, and efficiency measurements are overall credible, and there is no obvious fatal flaw in the logic or implementation as presented.
