Summary: The paper proposes Efficient Safe Policy Optimization (ESPO), a primal-based safe reinforcement learning method that adaptively manipulates sample size based on the conflict between reward and safety gradients. ESPO combines a three-mode optimization scheme—reward-only, cost-only, and joint reward-cost updates within a soft constraint region—with dynamic sample-size adjustment: larger batches when reward and safety gradients conflict, smaller batches otherwise. The paper provides convergence analysis in a tabular softmax-policy setting, argues reduced oscillation relative to CRPO/PCRPO, and presents experiments on Safety-MuJoCo and Omnisafe showing improved reward, safety, and reduced sample usage/training time versus several primal and primal-dual baselines.

Strengths: Addresses an important practical issue in safe RL: sample inefficiency under safety constraints.; The core idea is intuitive and potentially useful: use reward-cost gradient conflict as a signal for adaptive sampling.; Builds on a sensible three-mode optimization framework that distinguishes severe violation, soft violation, and safe regions.; Includes both theoretical and empirical components, which strengthens the overall contribution.; Experiments cover two commonly used benchmark suites and compare against both primal and primal-dual baselines.; Reported empirical gains are substantial in sample count and training time, with generally improved reward and safety curves.; The paper includes ablations on cost limits, sample-size settings, and update-style behavior.; The oscillation-reduction motivation is compelling, and the update-style statistics support the claim that ESPO spends more iterations optimizing reward within the safe region.

Weaknesses: The theoretical analysis is limited to a tabular softmax setting, while the experiments are in deep continuous-control domains; the gap between theory and practice is large.; The claimed sample-efficiency result is weakly formalized: Proposition 4.3 only states ESPO needs fewer samples than a non-adaptive variant, without a sharp quantitative complexity bound.; A key part of the sample-efficiency proof relies on Assumption A.7, which appears ad hoc and not rigorously justified from the RL estimation procedure.; There are apparent inconsistencies in the algorithm description: in Section 4.1 equations (3) and (4) are associated with conflict/non-conflict one way, while Section 4.3 appears to swap which update is used for angle <= 90° versus > 90°.; The notation and mode labels are sometimes confusing or inconsistent (e.g., Br/Bc naming versus mode descriptions), making the method harder to verify.; Experimental evaluation uses only at least three random seeds, which is relatively limited for high-variance RL benchmarks.; The paper emphasizes sample manipulation, but comparisons are mostly against methods without adaptive sampling; stronger baselines or adaptive-batch ablations would better isolate the contribution.; It is unclear whether improvements stem mainly from adaptive sample size, from the PCRPO-style three-mode update, or from implementation/hyperparameter advantages despite some ablations.; The practical overhead of computing reward and cost gradients and their angle each iteration is not deeply analyzed relative to the claimed efficiency gains.; Some writing and presentation issues reduce clarity, including grammatical errors and proof exposition that is difficult to follow.

Soundness Issues: There is a nontrivial soundness concern due to the apparent mismatch between the update rules described for gradient-conflict cases in Sections 4.1 and 4.3 / Algorithm 1. If the equations are indeed swapped, the implemented method may differ from the stated method.; The convergence theorem is inherited through substantial adaptation from prior CRPO-style analysis, but the proof presentation is hard to audit and contains notation inconsistencies, reducing confidence in full correctness.; The sample-efficiency claim depends on Assumption A.7 relating estimation error to sample size in a simplified linear form; this assumption is not established for the actual policy evaluation procedure used in deep RL experiments.; The theoretical guarantees do not directly justify the deep RL experimental setting, so claims of guaranteed convergence/stability for the practical algorithm are overstated if interpreted broadly.; Empirical claims are promising, but limited seed counts and lack of statistical significance testing weaken confidence that all reported gains are robust.
