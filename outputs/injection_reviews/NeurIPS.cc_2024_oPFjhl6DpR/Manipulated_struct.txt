Summary: The paper proposes Efficient Safe Policy Optimization (ESPO), a primal-based safe reinforcement learning method that adaptively manipulates sample size based on reward-cost gradient conflict within a three-mode optimization framework: reward-only, cost-only, and joint reward-cost updates. The method builds on PCRPO-style gradient handling and adds conflict-aware sample adjustment, increasing samples when reward and safety gradients conflict and decreasing them otherwise. The paper provides theoretical analysis in a tabular softmax-policy setting, including convergence guarantees comparable to CRPO, a proposition arguing reduced oscillation near the safe boundary, and a claim of improved sample efficiency from adaptive sampling. Empirically, ESPO is evaluated on Safety-MuJoCo and Omnisafe benchmarks against primal and primal-dual baselines, showing improved reward, better or comparable constraint satisfaction, and reduced sample usage/training time on the reported tasks.

Strengths: Addresses an important problem in safe RL: improving sample efficiency while maintaining constraint satisfaction.; The core idea is intuitive and potentially useful: use reward-safety gradient conflict as a signal for adaptive sample allocation.; Combines optimization-mode switching and adaptive sampling in a simple, implementable framework.; Includes both theoretical and empirical components, which strengthens the overall contribution.; Empirical results on multiple benchmark tasks show consistent gains in reported reward, safety, and wall-clock/sample efficiency over selected baselines.; The paper provides additional ablations on cost limits, sample-size settings, and update styles, which help illuminate the behavior of the method.; The discussion of oscillation near the safety boundary is insightful, and the update-style analysis supports the claimed behavioral difference from CRPO.

Weaknesses: The theoretical analysis is limited to a tabular softmax setting, whereas the experiments are in deep continuous-control settings; the gap between theory and practice is substantial.; The sample-efficiency claim is not especially rigorous: Proposition 4.3 is qualitative ('needs fewer samples') and relies on an additional assumption relating estimation error to sample size rather than a sharp end-to-end complexity bound.; Some theoretical statements appear loosely specified or potentially inconsistent, especially around notation, mode definitions, and the mapping between equations and algorithm branches.; The experimental comparison may not be fully controlled across baselines, since ESPO changes sample counts while some baselines are run with fixed budgets; stronger normalized comparisons at equal sample budgets would help.; The method introduces several hyperparameters/slack parameters (h+, h-, zeta+, zeta-, weights x_r/x_c), but guidance on tuning sensitivity and robustness is limited.; The paper sometimes overstates novelty and guarantees relative to what is formally established.; Writing clarity needs improvement: there are multiple notation issues, typos, and occasional confusion in the description of update rules and gradient-conflict cases.

Soundness Issues: There appears to be a mismatch in the algorithm description around the soft-constraint case: in Section 4.2/4.3, the text associates gradient-conflict and non-conflict cases with Equations (6)/(7) and update rules (3)/(4), but the mapping is described inconsistently across paragraphs and Algorithm 1.; The convergence theorem establishes a CRPO-like rate in a simplified tabular setting, but this does not directly justify the deep RL experimental regime; thus the theoretical support for the practical algorithm is only partial.; The sample-efficiency proposition depends on Assumption A.7 in the appendix, which is informal and not standard enough to fully substantiate the claimed improvement in sample complexity.; The oscillation-reduction proposition relies on adaptively choosing mixing parameters to maintain desirable behavior; it is not clear whether the practical algorithm specifies or computes these parameters in a way that guarantees the proposition’s conditions.; Empirical evidence is promising, but the paper does not provide enough statistical detail beyond using at least three seeds; significance testing or fuller variance reporting would improve confidence.; Comparisons in training time may be affected by implementation and benchmark differences, as the paper itself notes; therefore wall-clock claims should be interpreted cautiously.
