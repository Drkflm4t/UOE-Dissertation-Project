Summary: This paper studies semantic information flow inside GPT-style transformers using vocabulary-space projections of hidden states and weights (via the logit lens and related circuit-based projections). The authors analyze attention values, heads, and layer norms, arguing that attention outputs can be semantically traced from a small number of high-scoring memory values and dominant heads, especially when projecting through the OV circuit. Based on these observations, they introduce VISIT, an interactive flow-graph visualization tool that represents hidden states/neurons as nodes labeled by projected tokens and interactions as edges. The paper demonstrates the tool on GPT-2 through case studies on indirect object identification, layer norm behavior, and consistently activated 'regularization neurons.'

Strengths: Clear and interesting goal: making transformer internals more interpretable and accessible through an interactive visualization of semantic flow.; Novel emphasis on dynamic attention memory values and their relation to attention outputs, rather than only static weights or block-level hidden states.; The empirical observations about OV-projected attention heads, dominant high-norm heads, and top-attended memory values are intuitive and reasonably well supported by the presented analyses and figures.; The visualization framework is compelling and appears practically useful for qualitative analysis; the figures communicate complex internal computations in a readable way.; The IOI case study provides a helpful sanity check by qualitatively aligning the tool's outputs with prior mechanistic interpretability findings.; The paper offers several potentially interesting hypotheses beyond the tool itself, including layer norm as a semantic filter and the existence of always-active 'regularization neurons.'; The appendix provides substantial implementation detail, making the proposed graph construction relatively transparent and reproducible.

Weaknesses: Much of the evidence is correlational and based on logit-lens-style projection overlap, which is an imperfect proxy for semantic content and causal contribution.; The quantitative evaluation is limited in scale: many analyses use only 100 correctly answered CounterFact prompts and primarily GPT-2-medium, with only partial validation on GPT-2-XL.; Several claims are framed strongly despite modest evidence, especially regarding layer norm as a semantic filter and the interpretation of always-active neurons as regularization vectors.; The pruning strategy for the visualization relies on activation magnitude/norm heuristics, which may omit components that are causally important but not highly activated.; The paper does not provide a rigorous user study or task-based evaluation showing that VISIT improves interpretability, debugging, or research efficiency compared with prior tools.; Some analyses depend heavily on top-k token intersection (Ik), which may miss semantic similarity between different but related tokens and may be sensitive to arbitrary choices such as k=50.; Generalization beyond English GPT-style decoder-only models is not demonstrated.

Soundness Issues: The central methodology assumes that vocabulary projection of intermediate vectors faithfully reflects their semantics; while common in recent interpretability work, this assumption remains debated and is not independently validated here.; The use of Ik top-token overlap as the main semantic alignment metric is coarse and may overstate or understate meaningful relationships; alternative semantic similarity measures or robustness checks would strengthen the conclusions.; The claim that only a few dominant heads or top memory values determine semantics is supported by correlation with attention/block outputs, but not by causal interventions ablating those components.; The interpretation of layer norm as a semantic filter is intriguing, but the presented evidence mainly shows shifts in projected token probabilities before/after LN; this does not yet establish a mechanistic semantic role distinct from normalization effects.; The 'regularization neurons' claim is speculative: frequent activation, high norm, and high-entropy projections suggest an interesting phenomenon, but the proposed functional interpretation is not conclusively demonstrated.; The qualitative agreement with Wang et al. (2022) is encouraging, but it is not sufficient to validate the broader flow-graph methodology without more systematic comparisons or intervention-based tests.
