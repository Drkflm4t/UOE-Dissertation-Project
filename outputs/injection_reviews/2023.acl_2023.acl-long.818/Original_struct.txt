Summary: This paper presents a systematic empirical study of task-specific knowledge distillation for natural language generation under a realistic medium-resource setting with abundant unlabeled data. Across four English NLG tasks—summarization, question generation, abductive reasoning, and Shakespeare-to-modern-English style transfer—the authors compare architectural choices, pruning strategies, KD objectives, and pseudo-target (PT) training variants. The main contributions are: (1) an exposure-bias perspective on NLG distillation; (2) a broader PT training framework using unlabeled data, multiple PTs, and sampling-based PT generation; and (3) a new Joint-Teaching method that applies word-level KD on PTs generated by both teacher and student. Empirically, the study finds that encoder-decoder models outperform decoder-only models for task-specific conditional NLG, decoder pruning is preferable to encoder pruning for latency/quality tradeoffs, and PT-based KD—especially with unlabeled data, multiple sampled PTs, and Joint-Teaching—substantially improves distilled student performance, often recovering most of the teacher-student gap at large compression ratios. The paper also includes an exploratory extreme setup distilling GPT-4 with no labeled data, though results there are mixed.

Strengths: The paper addresses an important practical problem: compressing NLG models for deployment while assuming limited labeled data and plentiful unlabeled task-specific data, which is a realistic industrial setting.; The study is unusually systematic: it evaluates a sequence of design decisions spanning architecture, pruning, KD objective, PT usage, unlabeled data, decoding strategy, number of PTs, and Joint-Teaching.; The empirical scope is broad for an NLG KD paper, covering four distinct tasks and multiple model families (T5, BART, GPT2, OPT), plus both pruned and non-pruned students.; The exposure-bias motivation for PT training is insightful and helps unify several empirical choices; the analysis on page 5/Figure 2 provides some support for the intuition behind Joint-Teaching.; The proposed PT extensions are practically meaningful and well-motivated: using unlabeled data, multiple PTs, and sampling instead of only beam-search mode approximation.; Joint-Teaching is a simple and novel idea that appears effective empirically, winning many setups and further improving over teacher-only or student-only PT training.; The paper reports both task metrics and practical efficiency metrics such as latency and throughput, not just parameter counts or FLOPs; this strengthens the deployment relevance.; Results are generally strong and consistent: the final distilled models achieve large compression and latency gains while recovering a substantial fraction of teacher performance.; The paper is transparent about limitations and includes an exploratory section on distilling GPT-4 in a no-labeled-data regime, which broadens the paper’s relevance.; The appendix appears thorough, with implementation details, full tables, complexity discussion, and even a human evaluation for ART10 to probe metric limitations.

Weaknesses: The stage-wise experimental design selects one decision at a time based on average dev performance, which is practical but may miss important interactions between choices; some conclusions may therefore be contingent on earlier selections.; Most core claims are empirical rather than theoretically grounded; the exposure-bias argument is plausible but not rigorously formalized or causally isolated.; The study is limited to English-to-English tasks with relatively short outputs; generalization to longer-form generation, multilingual generation, NMT, or multimodal settings remains unclear.; The extreme GPT-4 setup is interesting but underdeveloped: it uses only two tasks, small sampled subsets, and mixed results, so it functions more as a pilot than a strong validation.; Training-time computational cost is largely excluded from the main comparison, even though some proposed methods—especially generating many PTs and Joint-Teaching—can be expensive.; Some methodological choices seem somewhat heuristic, such as fixing Joint-Teaching to 50% teacher/student PTs and using a linear stagewise search rather than stronger ablations over combinations.; The comparison of KD objectives is somewhat narrow; stronger or more recent sequence-level or intermediate-feature distillation baselines could further strengthen the claims.; The reported gains are aggregated as fractions of teacher-student gap closed, which is useful but can obscure absolute differences and variance across tasks/models.; The human evaluation for ART10 is helpful but small-scale, with only 50 examples and two annotators, limiting the strength of conclusions about automatic metric underestimation.; The paper occasionally overstates novelty, e.g., around exposure bias in KD and PT usage, where the framing is new but some ingredients are extensions of existing sequence-level KD practices rather than wholly new paradigms.

Soundness Issues: The methodology is generally sound and carefully executed, but the sequential decision pipeline introduces a risk of local-optimum conclusions: later-stage improvements are conditioned on earlier-stage selections and may not hold under alternative choices.; Because model/hyperparameter selection is based on average development performance across tasks, some per-task conclusions may be weaker than the aggregate narrative suggests.; The claim that PT training mitigates exposure bias is supported indirectly rather than through a direct causal evaluation; improvements could also stem from data augmentation, regularization, or better teacher coverage rather than exposure-bias reduction per se.; The GPT-4 logits KD setup relies on approximate tokenizer alignment and only top-5 teacher token probabilities, which weakens the fidelity of the distillation signal and makes conclusions from that section less robust.; The omission of training-cost accounting is acceptable given the stated deployment focus, but it weakens claims of practical superiority for methods requiring many PT generations.; Despite these caveats, the experiments are extensive, use dev-based model selection rather than test tuning, include multiple metrics and efficiency measures, and the main empirical conclusions for the realistic setup appear credible.
