Summary: This paper presents a systematic empirical study of task-specific knowledge distillation for natural language generation under a realistic medium-resource setting with abundant unlabeled data. Across four English NLG tasks—summarization, question generation, abductive reasoning, and Shakespeare-to-modern style transfer/simplification—the authors compare architectural choices, pruning strategies, KD objectives, and pseudo-target (PT) training variants. The main contributions are: (1) an exposure-bias perspective on NLG distillation; (2) a broader PT training framework that extends standard sequence-level KD to multiple PTs, sampling-based PT generation, and PTs for unlabeled data; and (3) a new Joint-Teaching method that applies word-level KD on PTs generated by both teacher and student. Empirically, the study finds that encoder-decoder models outperform decoder-only models for task-specific conditional NLG, decoder pruning is preferable to encoder pruning, and PT-based training—especially with unlabeled data, multiple sampled PTs, and Joint-Teaching—substantially improves distilled student performance while yielding strong latency/throughput gains. The paper also includes an exploratory extreme setup distilling GPT-4 into T5-small without labeled data.

Strengths: The paper addresses an important practical problem: compressing NLG models for deployment while preserving task performance.; The study is unusually systematic, covering architecture choice, pruning, KD objectives, PT generation strategies, unlabeled data usage, and a final extreme no-labeled-data setup.; The focus on realistic medium-resource task-specific settings with abundant unlabeled data is well motivated and practically relevant.; The exposure-bias framing for KD in NLG is insightful and helps motivate the PT-based methods conceptually.; The proposed PT extensions are simple, intuitive, and practically useful: multiple PTs, sampling-based PT generation, and PTs for unlabeled data.; Joint-Teaching is a novel and interesting idea that combines teacher- and student-generated PTs to address exposure bias more explicitly.; Experiments span multiple tasks rather than a single benchmark, improving the breadth of the conclusions.; The paper reports both task metrics and deployment-oriented efficiency metrics such as latency and throughput, which strengthens its practical value.; Results are generally consistent and substantial: unlabeled PTs, multiple PTs, and sampling each provide measurable gains, and the final distilled models achieve strong compression-speed tradeoffs.; The paper is transparent about limitations and includes extensive appendices, implementation details, and additional analyses, including human evaluation for ART10.

Weaknesses: The experimental design is largely sequential/greedy across stages rather than factorial, so interactions between design choices may be missed.; Many conclusions are based on averages over a limited set of tasks and model families, which may reduce generality beyond the studied English-to-English conditional NLG settings.; The proposed exposure-bias explanation is plausible but only partially validated; the supporting analysis is limited and does not establish causality.; Joint-Teaching uses a fixed 50/50 teacher-student PT mixture without much ablation on the mixing schedule or sensitivity to this hyperparameter.; The extreme GPT-4 setup is exploratory and yields mixed results, limiting the strength of conclusions there.; The tokenizer/logit alignment procedure in the GPT-4 distillation setup is somewhat heuristic and may weaken the validity of those results.; Training-time computational cost is explicitly not analyzed, even though some proposed methods require substantial offline PT generation and long KD runs.; Some comparisons may be influenced by implementation and hyperparameter choices fixed from earlier stages rather than jointly optimized per method.; The new Shake7 dataset is useful, but as a constructed dataset it would benefit from more discussion of quality control, diversity, and potential biases.; The paper focuses mainly on automatic metrics; broader human evaluation is limited to ART10.

Soundness Issues: The stage-wise selection procedure chooses one best option at each step based on development performance, which is practical but not methodologically ideal for isolating independent effects or interactions.; Claims about exposure bias mitigation are suggestive rather than rigorously demonstrated; the evidence from the page-5 analysis and downstream gains supports the intuition but does not conclusively verify the mechanism.; The GPT-4 logits KD setup relies on top-5 token probabilities and heuristic token alignment across different tokenizers, which introduces approximation error and weakens soundness for that section.; Because training cost is omitted from the main evaluation, claims of practical superiority should be interpreted primarily as inference-time efficiency gains rather than end-to-end efficiency.; That said, the core empirical methodology for the main realistic setup is generally solid: multiple tasks, clear baselines, dev-set-based model selection, comprehensive appendices, and consistent reporting of both quality and efficiency metrics.
