Summary: The paper develops general asymptotic lower and upper bounds on the KL divergence between a full Bayesian posterior and its coreset approximation, aiming to substantially broaden theory beyond prior analyses limited to exponential families or strongly log-concave/smooth models. The main lower-bound results (Theorems 3.3 and 3.5) relate unavoidable coreset error to mismatch in aggregate gradients and weight scaling under mild local regularity and posterior concentration assumptions. These are used to show that importance-weighted coresets require coreset size proportional to dataset size for bounded KL, even after optimal post-hoc scaling, and that any coreset with fewer than d support points cannot maintain bounded KL when the local likelihood geometry is d-dimensional. On the upper-bound side, the paper introduces a generalized subexponentiality condition on centered log-likelihood vectors and proves a KL upper bound in terms of a quadratic form in the weight error (Theorem 5.3), then applies it to subsample-optimize methods to argue bounded KL with polylogarithmic coreset size under an alignment condition. Empirical illustrations on two synthetic heavy-tailed/unidentifiable models support the qualitative theoretical predictions for importance-weighted and subsample-optimize constructions.

Strengths: Addresses an important gap in Bayesian coreset theory by providing both lower and upper bounds in settings beyond exponential families and strong log-concavity.; The lower-bound results are conceptually strong and practically meaningful: they explain why importance-sampling-based coresets can fail and establish a dimension-dependent minimum support size barrier.; The analysis is technically nontrivial yet organized around reusable tools (Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 5.1), which cleanly separate KL control from local posterior geometry and moment assumptions.; The assumptions for the lower bounds are relatively mild and aligned with standard Bayesian asymptotic reasoning, making the negative results broadly relevant.; The paper explicitly studies challenging posterior regimes—multimodality, unidentifiability, and heavy tails—where prior theory is weak, and the synthetic examples on pages 6, 8, and 10 visually support the claims.; Theoretical claims are generally well scoped, and proofs are provided in the appendix with clear theorem-to-corollary flow.; The upper-bound framework via subexponentiality is flexible and potentially useful beyond the specific applications discussed.

Weaknesses: The upper-bound application to subsample-optimize coresets relies on a strong and somewhat opaque alignment assumption (Eq. 6) whose verification is deferred to case-by-case analysis; this limits the practical force of the positive result.; The empirical section is narrow: only two synthetic models are studied, with no real-data experiments and no direct quantitative comparison against alternative coreset construction methods beyond the two analyzed families.; The optimization used in the subsample-optimize experiments appears to use a discretized least-squares surrogate based on posterior samples (page 10), whereas Algorithm 3 is stated as KL minimization; the gap between the analyzed algorithm and implemented approximation is not fully discussed.; Some asymptotic conclusions are qualitative (Op/Omega_p boundedness) rather than yielding explicit finite-sample constants or rates that practitioners could use to size coresets.; The paper focuses exclusively on KL divergence; while natural, it leaves unclear how the bounds translate to posterior expectations or downstream decision quality in non-asymptotic regimes.; The positive results are less compelling than the negative ones because the subexponential constant and alignment probability are not characterized generically, as acknowledged in the conclusion.

Methodological Flaws: The main theoretical development appears mathematically coherent from the provided proofs, and I did not identify a clear fatal error in scientific logic or proof structure. However, there is a methodological mismatch between the formal statement of Algorithm 3 (exact KL minimization over selected support) and the experimental implementation using nonnegative least squares on discretized log-likelihoods from posterior samples; this weakens the empirical validation of Corollary 6.1 because the experiments do not directly instantiate the analyzed optimization problem.; The empirical evidence is limited to synthetic settings and modest trial counts, so claims about broad practical effectiveness of subsample-optimize methods are only partially validated empirically; the experiments mainly illustrate consistency with asymptotic trends rather than establish robust empirical superiority.; Corollary 6.1 depends critically on the alignment probability condition in Eq. 6, but the paper does not provide a general mechanism showing this condition holds in the showcased models; thus the positive theorem is practically conditional in a way that is not empirically stress-tested.; The lower bounds use KL(w)=min{KL(pi_w||pi), KL(pi||pi_w)} while the upper bounds use KL(w)=max{...}; this is explicitly defined and not incorrect, but it creates some interpretational asymmetry that could confuse readers when comparing negative and positive results across sections.
