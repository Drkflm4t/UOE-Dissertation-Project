Summary: The paper develops general asymptotic lower and upper bounds on the KL divergence between a full Bayesian posterior and its coreset approximation, aiming to cover settings beyond prior theory for exponential families or strongly log-concave/smooth models. The main technical contributions are: (1) general KL lower bounds based on local posterior concentration and mismatch in weighted score/Hessian behavior (Theorems 3.3 and 3.5), (2) a general KL upper bound via a multivariate subexponentiality condition on centered log-likelihoods (Theorem 5.3), and (3) applications showing that importance-weighted coresets require size proportional to N to maintain bounded error, while subsample-optimize methods can achieve bounded error with polylogarithmic coreset size under additional assumptions. The theory is supported by experiments on two deliberately challenging models with multimodality, unidentifiability, and heavy tails, where empirical trends match the predicted asymptotic behavior.

Strengths: Addresses an important gap in Bayesian coreset theory by moving beyond restrictive assumptions such as exponential-family structure or strong log-concavity.; Provides both lower and upper bounds, which is more informative than papers that only prove algorithm-specific guarantees.; The lower-bound framework is conceptually strong: it reduces posterior approximation difficulty to analyzing weighted sums of score vectors, yielding clear impossibility results for importance-weighted constructions.; The negative result for importance sampling-based coresets is practically valuable and theoretically explains prior empirical failures.; The upper-bound framework via generalized subexponentiality is reasonably broad and appears weaker than assumptions used in prior work.; Applications to specific algorithms are meaningful: the paper derives concrete consequences for importance-weighted and subsample-optimize methods rather than leaving results at an abstract level.; Experiments are well aligned with the theory and use nontrivial models that violate common assumptions in prior analyses; the figures on pages 8 and 10 visually support the claimed asymptotic trends.; The paper is clearly written, technically organized, and includes full proofs in the appendix.

Weaknesses: Several key assumptions for the upper-bound application to subsample-optimize coresets remain difficult to verify in practice, especially the alignment condition in Eq. (6) and the subexponentiality constant/control in Proposition 5.4.; The main results are asymptotic and do not provide explicit finite-sample constants or practically computable error certificates, limiting immediate operational use.; The empirical section is relatively narrow, relying on two synthetic examples and existing algorithms; broader empirical validation on realistic models/datasets would strengthen the practical significance.; The upper-bound theory is somewhat indirect: Proposition 5.4 guarantees existence of a subexponential constant under local mgf finiteness, but this may still require substantial case-by-case work, as the paper itself acknowledges.; Some assumptions in the lower-bound applications, such as posterior concentration condition (A5) and moment/invertibility condition (A8), may be nontrivial in complex models and could limit the breadth of direct applicability.; The paper mainly analyzes KL divergence; discussion of how these bounds translate to downstream posterior expectations or credible intervals is limited.

Soundness Issues: Overall the logic appears sound and the proofs are supplied, with assumptions stated clearly for each theorem. The derivations connecting local quadratic approximations and concentration to KL lower bounds are plausible and technically careful.; The lower-bound applications seem methodologically solid: the importance-weighted impossibility result follows from asymptotic score mismatch of order M^{-1/2} versus N^{-1/2}, which is both intuitive and supported by the formal lemmas.; The upper-bound result is sound conditional on the subexponentiality assumption, but the practical strength of the theorem depends heavily on verifying that assumption and the associated constants in concrete models.; Corollary 6.1 for subsample-optimize methods is theoretically interesting but rests on a strong alignment probability assumption whose verification is deferred; thus the result is sound as a conditional theorem, but less decisive as a broadly established guarantee.; The experiments are appropriate as validation of asymptotic trends, though they do not stress-test the full generality of the theory.
