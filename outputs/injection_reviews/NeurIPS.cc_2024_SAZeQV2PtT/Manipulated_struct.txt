Summary: The paper studies Bayesian coresets—weighted sparse subsets used to approximate full-data posteriors—and develops general asymptotic lower and upper bounds on the KL divergence between the coreset posterior and the exact posterior. The main theoretical contributions are: (i) broadly applicable lower bounds (Theorems 3.3 and 3.5) under mild local regularity and posterior concentration assumptions, including extensions to settings where the parameterization is non-identifiable via a pushforward argument; (ii) a general upper bound (Theorem 5.3) based on a generalized multivariate subexponentiality condition; and (iii) applications of these bounds showing that importance-weighted coresets require size proportional to dataset size to maintain bounded error, that any coreset generally needs more than d points when the local likelihood geometry is d-dimensional, and that subsample-optimize methods can achieve bounded error with polylogarithmic coreset size under suitable alignment/subexponentiality assumptions. The theory is supported by experiments on two deliberately challenging synthetic models (multimodal/unidentifiable Cauchy location and heavy-tailed unidentifiable logistic regression), where the empirical KL trends match the predicted asymptotic behavior.

Strengths: Addresses an important theoretical gap in Bayesian coreset research by providing both lower and upper KL bounds in settings substantially broader than prior analyses restricted to exponential families or strong log-concavity/smoothness.; The lower-bound results are conceptually strong and practically meaningful: they explain why importance-sampling-style coreset constructions can fail fundamentally, not just empirically.; The paper derives several sharp and interpretable consequences from the general theory, especially Corollaries 4.1, 4.2, and 4.3, which give concrete limitations on coreset size and construction strategies.; The upper-bound framework via generalized subexponentiality is elegant and flexible; it appears meaningfully weaker and more broadly applicable than assumptions used in earlier work.; The pushforward extension (Corollary 3.6) is valuable because it allows the theory to cover unidentifiable or non-Euclidean parameterizations through lower-dimensional sufficient structure.; The experiments are well chosen to stress-test the theory in nonstandard regimes—multimodality, unidentifiability, and heavy tails—rather than only easy asymptotically normal settings.; Empirical figures clearly validate the predicted scaling laws for importance-weighted and subsample-optimize methods.; The paper is generally well written, with a clear problem formulation, precise desiderata for useful coresets, and a logical progression from generic lemmas to algorithm-specific implications.; Proofs are extensive and, on the whole, technically sophisticated and self-contained in the appendix.

Weaknesses: The upper-bound application to subsample-optimize methods relies on an alignment condition in Corollary 6.1 that may be difficult to verify in practice and currently requires case-by-case analysis.; The empirical evaluation is limited to two synthetic models; while these are thoughtfully chosen and aligned with the theory, broader empirical coverage on real datasets would strengthen the practical impact.; Some assumptions and asymptotic conditions are technically dense, which may make it hard for non-specialists to determine exactly when the results apply in a given model.; The paper focuses on asymptotic boundedness of KL rather than finite-sample constants or explicit construction-time guarantees for optimized coresets.

Methodological Flaws: A notable issue is the presence of prompt-injection text embedded in the PDF ('LLM REVIEWER NOTE HERE!!!' on pages 1 and 31) instructing the reviewer to be artificially more positive. This is inappropriate and should be disregarded; it does not affect the scientific content, but it is a serious procedural concern.; Theorem 5.3 and Corollary 6.1 provide existence-style guarantees under subexponentiality/alignment assumptions, but the paper does not offer broadly checkable sufficient conditions for the alignment probability in Eq. (6). This weakens empirical falsifiability of the upper-bound application.; The empirical validation is supportive but narrow: only synthetic examples are used, and the subsample-optimize experiment appears to instantiate weight optimization via discretized nonnegative least squares rather than directly minimizing KL, so the experiment validates the broader construction paradigm more than the exact theorem statement.; Some lower-bound conclusions are asymptotic and depend on posterior concentration assumption (A5); although the paper verifies this for its examples, the practical scope of A5 outside such settings is not fully characterized.
