Summary: This paper introduces a new task setting for open-world product attribute mining under light supervision: expanding values for known seed attribute types while also discovering values for previously unseen attribute types. To support this setting, the authors construct WOAM, a dataset spanning four Amazon product categories with titles and bullet-point descriptions, using a curated seed attribute set and human-annotated test data. The proposed method, Amacer, uses syntax-based candidate span generation, supervised contrastive learning from seed attributes, adaptive expansion for known attributes, DBSCAN for additional clustering, and two auxiliary regularizers for new-attribute discovery: a self-supervised bullet-point co-occurrence objective and an unsupervised latent-attribute reconstruction objective. Experiments show consistent gains over closed-world tagging baselines and OA-Mine-style open-world baselines, with the strongest reported result being 47.6 exact-match F1 / 59.1 partial-match F1, and notable improvements on unseen attribute types.

Strengths: The paper tackles a practically important and underexplored setting: open-world attribute mining without requiring explicit names/descriptions of unseen target attributes at inference time.; The task formulation is clear and meaningfully extends prior closed-world and zero-shot/open-world extraction settings.; The dataset contribution appears substantial: WOAM includes richer product context than prior title-only datasets, covers multiple categories, and explicitly measures unseen attribute types and values.; The proposed pipeline is well motivated, modular, and easy to follow; Figure 2 provides a clear overview of candidate generation, representation learning, and clustering.; The syntax-oriented candidate generation is simple, scalable, and empirically stronger than segmentation-based alternatives in this setting.; The use of supervised contrastive learning over seed attributes is sensible and appears more effective than prior triplet/regression formulations.; The self-supervised and unsupervised regularization components are novel in this task context and are supported by ablations showing gains, especially on new attribute types.; Experimental comparisons are fairly broad, including closed-world tagging baselines, OA-Mine variants, and ablations of the proposed method.; The paper includes useful decomposed analyses by seed vs. new attributes and title vs. bullet-point text, which strengthens the empirical story.; The qualitative examples and latent-attribute visualizations help illustrate both successes and remaining challenges.

Weaknesses: The evaluation protocol is somewhat unconventional: the main reported F1 is a pseudo-F1 derived from averaged clustering metrics plus recall, rather than a standard end-to-end extraction metric. This makes interpretation less direct.; The method is a pipeline with several heuristic components (POS-pattern candidate generation, adaptive thresholding, DBSCAN settings), so gains may depend on engineering choices rather than a unified principled objective.; Candidate generation quality remains a major bottleneck; Table 5 shows low span precision and exact-match F1, suggesting the overall system still struggles substantially at extraction.; The open-world discovery capability is still limited in absolute terms: performance on new attribute types remains much lower than on seed types, and recall on new types is modest.; The latent-attribute module is interesting but somewhat loosely connected to true attribute semantics; the paper itself notes repetition/topic collapse among learned latent attributes.; The dataset construction relies on product profiles plus human curation, which is practical, but may still encode biases from retailer-provided attributes and category-specific heuristics.; The scope is limited to four English-language product categories, so cross-domain and multilingual generalization remain untested.; Comparisons to stronger modern span proposal methods or generative extraction approaches are absent.; The paper does not deeply analyze sensitivity to key hyperparameters such as the number of latent attributes K, DBSCAN eps, or the adaptive expansion relaxation factor.; Because clustering is evaluated on non-overlapping spans with one label per span, the setup may underrepresent realistic cases where attribute mentions overlap or admit multiple plausible type assignments.

Soundness Issues: The methodology is generally reasonable, and the empirical improvements are consistent across tables, but the main evaluation metric is only an approximate proxy for precision/recall trade-offs in clustering-based extraction. This weakens the strength of claims about overall extraction quality.; The adaptive expansion threshold is heuristic, based on average intra-seed cosine similarity scaled by a tunable factor. While intuitive, there is limited justification that this threshold is stable across attributes with very different dispersion.; The self-supervised assumption that spans from the same bullet point tend to share similar attributes/topics is plausible but noisy; the paper acknowledges this, but does not quantify when this heuristic helps versus hurts.; The unsupervised latent-attribute objective borrows from topic modeling, but the correspondence between latent topics and product attributes is imperfect. Topic collapse/redundancy, acknowledged in the qualitative analysis, raises concerns about how reliably this module captures attribute structure.; The seed set is curated using existing retailer resources and manual consolidation. While this improves quality, it may also advantage methods tailored to those seed distributions; the paper does not fully disentangle dataset construction effects from modeling gains.; Statistical significance is claimed for the best F1, but the paper provides little detail on the testing protocol, variance across runs, or confidence intervals.
