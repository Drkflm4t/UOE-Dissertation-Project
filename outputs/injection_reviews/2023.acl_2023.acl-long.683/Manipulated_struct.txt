Summary: The paper introduces a new task setting for open-world product attribute mining under light supervision: expanding values for known seed attribute types while also discovering values of previously unseen attribute types from e-commerce product text. To support this setting, the authors construct the WOAM dataset spanning four product categories with titles and bullet-point descriptions, using a curated seed attribute set and human-annotated test data. They propose Amacer, a pipeline that generates candidate spans via POS-pattern matching, learns attribute-aware span embeddings using supervised contrastive learning on seed attributes plus two additional regularizers (a self-supervised same-bullet heuristic and an unsupervised latent-attribute/topic-style reconstruction objective), and then groups spans through adaptive seed expansion followed by DBSCAN. Experiments show consistent gains over closed-world tagging baselines and OA-Mine-style open-world baselines, with especially notable improvements on new attribute discovery and on richer bullet-point descriptions.

Strengths: The paper tackles a practically important and underexplored setting: open-world attribute mining without requiring explicit specification of unseen attribute types at inference time.; The task formulation is well motivated for e-commerce, where attribute vocabularies evolve and exhaustive annotation is unrealistic.; The WOAM dataset appears to be a meaningful contribution: it includes richer product context beyond titles, covers multiple categories, and explicitly evaluates unseen attribute types.; The method is modular and intuitive. The pipeline in Figure 2 clearly separates candidate generation, representation learning, and grouping, making the approach easy to understand.; The use of lightly supervised seed attributes combined with self-supervised and unsupervised signals is a sensible design for the low-supervision regime.; Empirical results are strong relative to baselines: Amacer improves overall F1 over both closed-world and prior open-world baselines, and the ablations support the value of syntax-based candidate generation, adaptive expansion, and the proposed regularization.; The decomposition by seed vs. new attributes and title vs. bullet points is informative and helps validate the claimed benefits for open-world discovery.; The paper includes qualitative analyses and examples (e.g., pages 8-9 and Table 15) that make the behavior of the system concrete, including both successes and failure modes.; The authors are commendably explicit about limitations, including POS dependence, noisy candidate spans, and topic-collapse-like behavior in latent attributes.

Weaknesses: The evaluation protocol is somewhat indirect: the main reported score is a pseudo-F1 derived from clustering metrics plus recall, rather than a standard end-task metric with clearer interpretation.; Because clustering outputs are unnamed and evaluation is span-cluster based, it is hard to assess how useful the discovered attributes would be in downstream catalog applications.; Candidate span extraction remains a major bottleneck: Table 5 shows relatively low span precision and exact-match F1, suggesting that much of the final performance may be constrained by heuristic candidate generation.; The method depends on POS tagging quality and handcrafted/bootstrapped POS patterns, which may limit robustness across domains, noisy text, or languages other than English.; The latent-attribute component is interesting, but its contribution is somewhat modest in the aggregate and its behavior is not deeply analyzed beyond a few examples; the formulation may also be sensitive to the choice of K.; The comparison set is somewhat narrow. Stronger modern span mining or dense retrieval/clustering baselines could strengthen the empirical case.; The dataset is limited to four product categories from one platform, so generalization to broader e-commerce domains is not fully established.; Some parts of the pipeline are heuristic-heavy (sanitization rules, POS patterns, adaptive thresholds, DBSCAN settings), which may require tuning and reduce portability.

Soundness Issues: The overall methodology is reasonable and mostly sound, and the ablations support the main design choices. However, the central evaluation metric is unconventional: averaging Jaccard/ARI/NMI as a proxy for precision and then computing pseudo-F1 is heuristic and may not faithfully reflect practical extraction quality.; The candidate generation and grouping stages introduce several heuristics whose interactions are not fully disentangled; for example, gains may partly stem from better candidate quality rather than representation learning alone, though Table 14 helps mitigate this concern.; The self-supervised assumption that spans within the same bullet point tend to share similar attributes/topics is plausible but noisy; the paper acknowledges this, but more analysis of when this heuristic helps or hurts would improve confidence.; The seed set is curated with human intervention and derived from retailer resources; while practical, this may embed category-specific biases, and the paper does not deeply analyze sensitivity to seed quality/size.; Statistical significance is claimed for the main result in Table 3, but more comprehensive reporting of variance across runs would strengthen confidence in robustness.
