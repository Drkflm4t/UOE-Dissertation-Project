Summary: The paper introduces a new task setting for open-world product attribute mining under light supervision, where the goal is both to expand known seed attribute types and to discover previously unseen attribute types from e-commerce product text. To support this setting, the authors construct a new dataset, WOAM, spanning four product categories with titles and bullet-point descriptions, and with substantial unseen attribute coverage in the test set. They propose Amacer, a pipeline consisting of syntax-based candidate span generation, supervised contrastive representation learning from a curated seed attribute set, adaptive expansion for known attributes, DBSCAN-based clustering for additional coverage, and two auxiliary regularizers for new-attribute discovery: a self-supervised bullet-point co-occurrence objective and an unsupervised latent-attribute reconstruction objective. Experiments show consistent gains over closed-world tagging baselines and OA-Mine-style open-world baselines, with the strongest reported result being 47.6 exact-match F1 and 59.1 partial-match F1 on their clustering-based evaluation, plus improved performance on unseen attribute types.

Strengths: The paper tackles a practically important and underexplored problem setting: open-world attribute mining without requiring explicit specification of new attribute types at inference time.; The task formulation is well motivated for e-commerce, where attribute vocabularies evolve rapidly and exhaustive annotation is unrealistic.; The WOAM dataset appears to be a meaningful contribution: it includes richer product context than prior title-only datasets, covers 66 attribute types with 42.4% unseen types in the test set, and uses a hybrid seed-construction process intended to improve supervision quality.; The proposed pipeline is modular and intuitive, combining candidate generation, representation learning, and clustering in a way that matches the weakly supervised setting.; The syntax-oriented candidate generation is a sensible alternative to segmentation-based approaches and is empirically shown to improve quality and efficiency.; The use of supervised contrastive learning over seed attributes is appropriate and simpler than prior triplet/regression formulations, with strong empirical gains.; The self-supervised and unsupervised regularization components are novel in this context and are well aligned with the goal of improving discovery of unseen attributes.; Experimental comparisons are fairly broad, including closed-world tagging baselines, OA-Mine variants, ablations, and decomposed analyses on seed vs. new attributes and titles vs. bullet points.; The paper includes useful qualitative analysis, including examples of successful seed expansion, failure cases for new attributes, and interpretable latent attributes.; The work is reproducible in spirit: the paper reports implementation details, releases code/data, and provides extensive appendix statistics and examples.

Weaknesses: The main evaluation metric is a custom pseudo-F1 derived from averaging clustering metrics (Jaccard, ARI, NMI) as a proxy for precision and combining that with recall. This metric is unconventional and its interpretation is not fully justified.; The candidate generation stage is heavily dependent on POS tagging quality and handcrafted pattern matching, which may limit robustness across domains, languages, or noisier text.; The empirical gains on new-attribute discovery, while meaningful, still leave performance relatively modest on unseen types (e.g., partial-match F1 46.2), indicating the task remains far from solved.; The dataset covers only four product categories, so it is unclear how well the approach generalizes to a broader range of e-commerce domains.; Comparisons to stronger modern span-generation or phrase-mining baselines are limited; the candidate generation design may benefit from broader benchmarking.; The paper acknowledges noisy extracted spans and topic/attribute collapse in the latent-attribute module, but does not deeply analyze their impact or mitigation.

Methodological Flaws: There is a likely mathematical issue in the supervised and self-supervised contrastive loss formulas: the denominator appears to sum only over negative examples, excluding the positive term that is normally included in InfoNCE/supervised contrastive objectives. If implemented exactly as written, the log-ratio can become positive and the objective no longer matches the standard probabilistic contrastive formulation. This could be a notation error, but as written it is scientifically incorrect and should be clarified.; The adaptive expansion threshold uses the average pairwise cosine similarity among seeds as a distance threshold surrogate, but the notation mixes 'distance' and 'similarity' inconsistently. Eq. (1) defines D using cosine(ci, sk) despite describing it as cosine distance, and Eq. (2) similarly uses cosine similarity. This ambiguity makes the grouping rule hard to verify mathematically.; The unsupervised latent-attribute model defines an Attribute-to-Span distribution over the set of unique candidate spans in a training batch rather than a fixed vocabulary, which makes the generative interpretation somewhat ad hoc and batch-dependent. This weakens the scientific grounding of the latent-variable formulation.; The evaluation protocol conflates clustering quality and extraction quality in a nonstandard way. Using the average of Jaccard/ARI/NMI as 'pseudo precision' is heuristic rather than theoretically justified, so improvements in the headline F1 may be sensitive to this design choice.; The paper reports statistical significance for the best F1 in Table 3, but the testing protocol is underspecified: it is unclear over what random variables or repeated runs the t-test is computed, given that results are macro-averaged across only four categories.; The seed set is curated using existing retailer attributes plus human consolidation, and development data is also derived from sanitized profile attributes. While practical, this creates a risk that the method is partially tuned to the structure of retailer-provided attributes, which may overestimate performance relative to truly unconstrained open-world discovery.
