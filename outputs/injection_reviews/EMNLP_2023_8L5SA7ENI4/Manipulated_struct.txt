Summary: This paper presents a critical survey of sentiment analysis (SA) from a sociotechnical and interdisciplinary perspective. Reviewing 189 papers spanning applications, models, datasets, surveys, and frameworks, the authors argue that SA research rarely defines either 'sentiment' or 'sentiment analysis' explicitly, often conflates sentiment with opinion, emotion, and subjectivity, and lacks standardized measurement frameworks. The paper analyzes 60 application papers, 43 models, and 19 datasets, highlighting inconsistent definitions, heterogeneous metrics, limited transparency/accessibility of public SA systems, and documented demographic biases. Based on these findings, it proposes four recommendations and a 10-question ethics sheet intended to guide more transparent, socially informed, and equitable development and deployment of SA systems.

Strengths: Timely and important topic: the paper addresses a widely used NLP task whose conceptual foundations and social implications are often underexamined.; Broad scope: the survey covers 189 papers across applications, models, datasets, surveys, and frameworks, giving a useful panoramic view of the field.; Clear central thesis: the paper convincingly argues that SA lacks conceptual clarity and interdisciplinary grounding.; Useful synthesis of technical and social perspectives: the contrast between computational notions of sentiment and psychological/sociological notions is valuable.; The application taxonomy (page 5 / Table 3 and Figure 2) is helpful and shows where SA is being deployed in practice, especially in health/medicine and government/policy.; The analysis of accessibility/transparency of models and datasets is practically relevant, especially for AI-as-a-service settings.; The discussion of bias and harm is important and supported with concrete examples, including the TextBlob illustration on page 7.; The proposed ethics sheet is a pragmatic artifact that could be useful for practitioners and reviewers.; The paper is generally well organized, moving from conceptual background to empirical survey findings to recommendations.

Weaknesses: The paper is framed as a critical survey, but the methodology for paper collection, inclusion/exclusion, coding, and analysis is not rigorous enough for a high-confidence systematic review.; Many claims are based on counts from manual review, but inter-annotator agreement statistics or more formal reliability measures are not reported.; The criteria for selecting the 43 'well-cited' models and 19 datasets are underspecified, which may introduce sampling bias.; Some arguments overreach from absence of explicit definitions in papers to broader conclusions about the field's conceptual incoherence; implicit operationalizations may exist even when not formally defined.; The survey emphasizes conceptual inconsistency, but does not sufficiently distinguish between task variants where different formulations are expected (e.g., polarity classification, emotion detection, aspect-based sentiment analysis).; The proposed ethics sheet is sensible but not validated through user studies, expert feedback, or case studies demonstrating its effectiveness.; The paper sometimes treats standardization as an unqualified good, but some diversity in metrics/frameworks may be appropriate across domains and tasks.; There are occasional writing/grammar issues and some imprecision in terminology that reduce polish.

Soundness Issues: The literature search process is only partially specified. Although sources and keywords are mentioned, the protocol lacks enough detail for full reproducibility, including exact search strings, dates, screening criteria, and deduplication procedures.; The thematic analysis of applications is described at a high level, but coding procedures, adjudication details, and reliability metrics are missing, limiting confidence in the reported categories and counts.; The inference that lack of explicit definitions directly causes bias or harmful deployment is plausible but not established causally by the survey evidence.; The accessibility analysis uses coarse indicators (code availability, dataset availability, paywall/access restrictions), which are useful but do not fully capture transparency or explainability.; The TextBlob case study is illustrative rather than systematic; it supports the paper's concerns but should not be taken as strong empirical evidence about SA systems broadly.; The paper mixes critique of sentiment analysis as a concept with critique of specific implementations and deployment practices; these are related but analytically distinct issues that could be separated more carefully.
