Summary: This paper studies hybrid reinforcement learning in episodic linear MDPs, where an agent can use both an offline dataset and online interaction without assuming single-policy concentrability. It proposes two computationally efficient algorithms: RAPPEL, an online-to-offline method that performs reward-agnostic exploration and then pessimistic offline RL for PAC policy learning; and HYRULE, an offline-to-online method that warm-starts an optimistic online RL algorithm for regret minimization. The main theoretical claims are that RAPPEL achieves PAC/error bounds that are never worse than the best known offline-only guarantees and can improve them depending on the offline/online partition, while HYRULE achieves regret bounds never worse than minimax-optimal online-only guarantees and can improve them when offline data is useful. The paper also provides numerical experiments on a scaled-down Tetris environment suggesting empirical benefits of both approaches over purely offline or purely online baselines. Overall, the work aims to show that hybrid RL can provably beat offline-only or online-only sample barriers in linear MDPs without strong coverage assumptions on the behavior policy.

Strengths: Addresses an important and timely question in hybrid RL: whether one can provably improve over offline-only and online-only guarantees in linear MDPs without single-policy concentrability.; Provides two complementary algorithmic perspectives, one for PAC learning (RAPPEL) and one for regret minimization (HYRULE), which makes the contribution broader than prior work focused on only one regime.; Theoretical guarantees are clearly positioned against prior upper and lower bounds; Table 2 and the surrounding discussion make the claimed improvements easy to understand.; The algorithms are built from known efficient components (OPTCOV, LinPEVI-ADV+, LSVI-UCB++), so the methods are computationally grounded rather than purely existential.; The partition-based analysis using offline and online subspaces is conceptually interesting and appears to sharpen dependence from ambient dimension d to quantities involving don and coff(Xoff).; The paper includes explicit discussion of practical distinctions between online-to-offline and offline-to-online hybrid RL, which helps motivate why both formulations matter.; The numerical experiments, though limited, are aligned with the theory and illustrate benefits under poor offline coverage and for warm-started online learning.

Weaknesses: The presentation is mathematically dense and at times difficult to follow; many key arguments are deferred to appendices, making it hard to assess the main proof ideas from the core text alone.; Several guarantees rely on partition-dependent quantities such as don and coff(Xoff), but the practical procedure for selecting or estimating a good partition is underdeveloped and somewhat heuristic.; The burn-in/sample-size requirements are very large, especially for HYRULE (e.g., Non, Noff = \tilde\Omega(d^13 H^14)), which substantially weakens the practical significance of the asymptotic improvements.; Some claimed improvements are relative to worst-case bounds but still leave substantial gaps, especially in horizon dependence and in the strong polynomial prefactors hidden by \tilde O notation.; The empirical evaluation is small-scale, limited to one toy-like domain, and does not thoroughly test sensitivity to key quantities such as offline data quality, horizon, feature dimension, or partition choice.; Algorithm 1 depends on reward-agnostic exploration via OPTCOV and a tolerance choice tied to unknown structural quantities, which may be difficult to tune in practice.; The paper occasionally overstates practical impact relative to the very restrictive linear MDP setting and the heavy assumptions needed for the theory.

Soundness Issues: The overall methodology is plausible and builds on established analyses from prior work, but the strongest claims depend on intricate reductions and appendixed lemmas that are hard to fully verify from the main text alone.; Assumption 2 (full-rank projected covariates) is important for RAPPEL and may be nontrivial; while the paper argues it can be achieved via feature transformation, this deserves more careful justification because it affects the scope of the result.; The practical meaning of the infimum over partitions in the regret bound is somewhat delicate: the algorithm is partition-unaware, but the guarantee is analyzed through an existential partition, which is theoretically valid if the proof is correct but may obscure what structure is actually exploited algorithmically.; The very large lower-order and burn-in terms suggest that the asymptotic improvements may only appear in regimes far beyond realistic sample sizes; this does not invalidate the theory but limits the strength of practical conclusions.; There is a minor presentation concern where the authors note a likely typo in a cited prior paper and modify the variance threshold accordingly; this seems reasonable, but such dependence on correcting prior notation should be handled carefully.
