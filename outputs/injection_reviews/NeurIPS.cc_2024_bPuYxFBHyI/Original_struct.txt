Summary: The paper studies hybrid reinforcement learning in episodic linear MDPs, where an agent has access to both an offline dataset and online interaction. It proposes two computationally efficient algorithms: (1) RAPPEL, an online-to-offline method that uses reward-agnostic exploration (via OPTCOV) to augment offline data and then runs pessimistic offline RL; and (2) HYRULE, an offline-to-online method that warm-starts an optimistic online RL algorithm using offline data. The main claims are improved PAC/error and regret bounds over prior hybrid RL results in linear MDPs without requiring single-policy concentrability, with guarantees that are at least as good as offline-only or online-only baselines and can be strictly better depending on the partition of the feature space into well-covered and poorly covered components. The paper also includes small-scale experiments on a projected-feature Tetris environment intended to illustrate improved coverage, policy value, and regret from combining offline and online data.

Strengths: Addresses an important open question in hybrid RL theory: whether hybrid RL can provably improve over offline-only and online-only guarantees in linear MDPs without single-policy concentrability.; Covers both major hybrid RL paradigms: online-to-offline PAC learning and offline-to-online regret minimization, which broadens the paper's conceptual contribution.; Theoretical results are ambitious and potentially impactful: the bounds in Table 2 improve prior hybrid RL bounds in horizon dependence and adapt to offline/online partitions through quantities such as coff(Xoff) and don.; Algorithms are computationally grounded in existing efficient primitives (OPTCOV, LinPEVI-ADV+, LSVI-UCB++), rather than being purely information-theoretic.; The discussion clearly positions the work relative to prior literature, and Table 1/Table 2 are useful for contextualizing contributions.; The experiments, though limited, are aligned with the paper's narrative: they compare hybrid vs offline-only vs online-only coverage/value/regret and show qualitative benefits even with poor offline data.

Weaknesses: The paper's central guarantees depend on a partition Xoff/Xon of the state-action-feature space, but this partition is not operationally specified in a way that makes the bounds directly actionable. For RAPPEL, the user must effectively choose tolerance/don; for HYRULE, the algorithm is partition-unaware but the guarantee is existential/infimum-based.; Several claims are framed as improvements over lower bounds or as 'breaking sample size barriers,' but the comparison is not fully apples-to-apples because the bounds involve new partition-dependent quantities rather than standard minimax parameters alone.; The burn-in/sample requirements are extremely large and impractical (e.g., Non,Noff = \tilde\Omega(d^{13}H^{14}) for HYRULE), which substantially weakens the practical significance of the asymptotic improvements.; The empirical section is very limited: only one environment, low trial counts, simplified implementations that substitute core subroutines, and no direct empirical validation of the claimed scaling with d, H, offline coverage quality, or partition quantities.; Some assumptions and proof steps are insufficiently justified at a conceptual level, especially the practical meaning and attainability of Assumption 2 for arbitrary partitions and the reliance on feature projections derived from offline covariance.; The presentation occasionally overstates the strength of the results; e.g., 'tightest theoretical guarantees currently available' is plausible but not rigorously substantiated beyond selected comparisons.

Methodological Flaws: There is a notable scientific-logic gap between the theorem statements and algorithmic usability. The main bounds optimize over or depend on partitions Xoff/Xon, but the algorithms do not generally construct these partitions. For RAPPEL, the paper acknowledges the need to choose tolerance/don via SVD heuristics; thus the strongest guarantees are not fully algorithmic but partly oracle/ex post in nature.; Assumption 2 ('Full Rank Projected Covariates') is presented as mild, but it effectively requires finite partial coverability for any partition and is justified by projecting onto nonzero singular directions of the offline covariance. This justification is not fully convincing: offline covariance eigenspaces need not certify online explorability of arbitrary projected subspaces under the MDP dynamics. The logical leap from feature-space rank reduction to existence of an exploration policy with uniformly positive covariance at every stage is not rigorously established in the main text.; Lemma 1 / Lemma 5 style arguments bound con(Xon) by don via Kiefer-Wolfowitz and then use this to motivate OPTCOV tolerance choices. However, the theorem statements compare against arbitrary partitions, while the proof of existence of a good partition for coff(Xoff)=O(d) is weak for practical purposes and does not imply simultaneous favorable values of coff(Xoff) and don for the same partition. This weakens the interpretation that the method universally improves over standard rates.; The empirical validity is limited by implementation deviations explicitly stated in the experiments: Algorithm 2 uses LSVI-UCB++ 'as-is', while Algorithm 1 substitutes LSVI-UCB for FORCE within OPTCOV and LinPEVI-ADV for LinPEVI-ADV+. Therefore the experiments do not directly evaluate the proposed algorithms as analyzed, reducing the evidentiary value of the empirical section.; The regret theorem for HYRULE requires very large lower bounds on Non,Noff and hides substantial additive terms before concluding the final rate. While asymptotically acceptable, this raises concern that the claimed improvement may only emerge in regimes far beyond realistic sample sizes; the paper does not quantify this transition empirically or theoretically.; The paper points out an apparent typo in He et al. (2023) and modifies the variance floor from H to sqrt(H). This may be correct, but because HYRULE's analysis depends on this correction, the paper should more carefully isolate which guarantees rely on the amended definition and whether the original source's theorem can indeed be imported unchanged under this modification.
