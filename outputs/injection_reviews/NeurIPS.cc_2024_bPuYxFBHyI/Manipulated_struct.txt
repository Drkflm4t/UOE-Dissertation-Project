Summary: The paper studies hybrid reinforcement learning in linear MDPs, where an agent has access to both an offline dataset and online interaction. It proposes two computationally efficient algorithms targeting the two main hybrid-RL paradigms: (1) RAPPEL, an online-to-offline method that performs reward-agnostic exploration and then runs pessimistic offline RL to output a PAC policy; and (2) HYRULE, an offline-to-online method that warm-starts an optimistic online RL algorithm using offline data to minimize regret. The main theoretical claim is that both algorithms achieve guarantees that are never worse than the best known offline-only or online-only rates and can strictly improve them without requiring single-policy concentrability. The paper also provides numerical experiments on a scaled-down Tetris environment suggesting empirical benefits of both approaches.

Strengths: Addresses an important open question in hybrid RL: whether one can provably improve over offline-only and online-only sample barriers in linear MDPs without single-policy concentrability.; Introduces two conceptually clean and computationally efficient algorithms covering both major hybrid-RL settings: PAC learning via online-to-offline exploration and regret minimization via offline-to-online warm start.; Theoretical guarantees are strong and appear to improve the prior state of the art for hybrid RL in linear MDPs, as summarized in Table 2.; The decomposition into offline and online partitions is elegant and gives interpretable bounds in terms of partial coverage quantities (coff and don), clarifying when hybrid data helps.; The paper makes technically nontrivial progress on horizon dependence, obtaining H^3-type dependence in key terms via truncation and total-variance arguments.; The work is well positioned relative to prior literature, with clear comparisons to offline RL, online RL, and prior hybrid RL results.; The numerical experiments, though modest, are aligned with the theory and illustrate benefits under both favorable and adversarial offline data conditions.; The discussion section is thoughtful about limitations and future directions, including burn-in costs and the challenge of obtaining a single algorithm that simultaneously dominates both offline-only and online-only rates.

Weaknesses: The presentation is mathematically dense, and several key quantities and assumptions (especially the partition choice and practical selection of don / tolerance in RAPPEL) may be difficult for readers to operationalize.; The empirical evaluation is limited to a single scaled-down Tetris domain with relatively small-scale experiments, so practical robustness across environments is not yet established.; Algorithm 1 requires choosing a partition/tolerance indirectly through don or spectral inspection of offline data, which weakens the plug-and-play nature of the method.; Some guarantees require large polynomial burn-in/sample thresholds (e.g., Non, Noff = \tilde\Omega(d^{13}H^{14}) for HYRULE), which may limit practical relevance of the asymptotic improvements.; The paper occasionally relies on informal explanatory statements where sharper clarification would help, especially around the existence/selection of favorable partitions and the interpretation of partial concentrability/coverability.

Since lambda_{doff} can be arbitrarily small for poorly chosen partitions, the statement on page 7 that 'there exists at least one partition such that coff(Xoff)=O(d)' is plausible, but several later comparisons and informal remarks read as if both coff(Xoff) and don can simultaneously be taken <= d for a useful partition without tradeoff. This should be stated more carefully, since the optimization over partitions is central to the claimed gains.; The proof of Lemma 5 appears to use Matrix Chernoff on the offline covariance and then concludes max_{phi in Phi} phi^T Lambda_h^{-1} phi \lesssim max{coff(Xoff)/Noff, don/Non}. However, the argument mixes guarantees over Phi_off and Phi_on and then extends to all Phi via 'As Lambda_{h,off}+Lambda_{h,on}=Lambda_h'. This step is intuitive but under-explained; because the maximization is over the union/subspace structure, a more explicit argument is needed to justify the bound for arbitrary phi in Phi rather than separately projected components.; For HYRULE, the stated regret theorem requires Non, Noff = \tilde\Omega(d^{13}H^{14}), which is extremely large. While not invalid, this weakens the practical meaning of the asymptotic improvement claim and should be emphasized more prominently in the main theorem statement/discussion.; The empirical section does not directly validate the scaling predicted by the theory. It shows qualitative gains, but does not test dependence on Noff, Non, d, H, or coverage quality in a way that would substantiate the claimed sample-complexity improvements.; There is a possible notation/definition issue around the regret definition in Section 2.1: Reg_L(T) is defined using expectation over rewards and initial states, but later theorems state high-probability regret bounds without always clearly distinguishing pseudo-regret from realized regret.
