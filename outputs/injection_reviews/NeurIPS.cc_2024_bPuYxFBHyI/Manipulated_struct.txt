Summary: The paper studies hybrid reinforcement learning in linear MDPs, where an agent can use both an offline dataset and online interaction. It proposes two computationally efficient algorithms: RAPPEL, an online-to-offline method that performs reward-agnostic exploration and then pessimistic offline RL for PAC policy learning; and HYRULE, an offline-to-online method that warm-starts an optimistic online RL algorithm for regret minimization. The main theoretical claim is that both algorithms achieve guarantees that are never worse than the best known offline-only or online-only rates and can strictly improve on them without requiring single-policy concentrability. The paper also provides finite-sample bounds in terms of partition-dependent quantities capturing offline coverage and online explorable dimensions, compares against prior work in Table 2, and includes small-scale experiments on a Tetris-like environment showing empirical benefits of combining offline and online data.

Strengths: Addresses an important open question in hybrid RL with function approximation, extending beyond the tabular setting to linear MDPs.; Introduces two conceptually distinct and practically relevant hybrid paradigms: online-to-offline PAC learning and offline-to-online regret minimization.; Provides computationally efficient algorithms rather than purely information-theoretic results.; Theoretical guarantees are strong and clearly positioned relative to prior work; Table 2 gives a useful comparison to offline-only, online-only, and prior hybrid results.; Removes the need for single-policy concentrability assumptions, which is a meaningful advance over much of the prior literature.; The partition-based analysis is interesting and yields adaptive bounds in terms of offline coverage and online explorable subspaces.; For HYRULE, the algorithm is partition-unaware yet the regret bound adapts to the best partition in the analysis.; The paper includes substantial technical development, including sharper dimension dependence and improved horizon dependence relative to prior hybrid linear-MDP results.; Experiments, though limited, are aligned with the theory and illustrate benefits under poor offline behavior policies.; The writing is generally clear for a theory paper, with contributions and positioning stated explicitly in the introduction and discussion.

Weaknesses: The main guarantees depend on partition-dependent quantities such as coff(Xoff) and don, which may be difficult to estimate or optimize in practice.; Algorithm 1 requires choosing a tolerance / effectively choosing don, and the practical prescription via SVD is heuristic rather than theoretically resolved.; The burn-in/sample-size requirements are very large, especially for HYRULE where the theorem assumes Non, Noff = \u03a9~(d^13 H^14), which substantially limits practical relevance of the finite-sample statement.; Some bounds still fall short of the cleanest conjectured forms; for example the paper explicitly notes it does not achieve the desired sqrt(coff(Xoff)dH^3/Noff)+sqrt(don d H^3/Non) style bound for Algorithm 1 in full generality.; The empirical section is small-scale and uses simplified implementations/substitutions rather than the exact proposed algorithms, which weakens experimental support.; Experiments are limited to one environment family and a small number of trials, so they do not strongly validate robustness or scaling.; The practical meaning of the partition Xoff/Xon framework may be somewhat abstract, and the paper does not provide an end-to-end method for selecting or learning the partition.; Some assumptions, especially the projected full-rank/explorability assumption for RAPPEL, may still be restrictive or at least nontrivial to verify in realistic problems.; There is no lower-bound contribution establishing optimality of the new hybrid rates under the proposed partition-dependent measures.; The presentation is mathematically dense, and some proof ideas rely on intricate truncation arguments that may be hard to verify without careful checking.

Soundness Issues: The overall logical structure is sound: the algorithms are built from established components (OPTCOV, LinPEVI-ADV+, LSVI-UCB++) and the claimed improvements come from a partitioned analysis plus new technical lemmas.; Theorems appear to be stated with explicit assumptions and are supported by detailed appendices. The comparison claims are mostly careful and qualified as improvements over existing upper bounds or lower bounds under stated regimes.; A notable concern is the very large polynomial burn-in/sample assumptions, especially for HYRULE, which make the theoretical improvement somewhat asymptotic and may hide substantial constants.; Algorithm 1's practical applicability depends on selecting the exploration tolerance/partition appropriately; while the theorem is valid conditionally, the methodology for this choice is not fully integrated into the theory.; The experiments do not fully validate the exact algorithms analyzed, since the paper states on page 12 that simplified substitutes are used for implementation. This does not invalidate the theory, but it weakens empirical confirmation.; There is a minor presentation concern around the claimed typo correction in He et al. (2023); while plausible, such corrections should ideally be handled more cautiously or independently justified.; The claim that the guarantees are 'no worse than' offline-only or online-only rates is broadly supported by choosing extreme partitions, though the practical attainability of the best partition-dependent gains is less clear.
