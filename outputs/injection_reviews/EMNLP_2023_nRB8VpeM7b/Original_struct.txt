Summary: This paper proposes Pushdown Layers, a drop-in replacement for Transformer self-attention that augments language models with an explicit stack-like memory encoded as a "stack tape" of token depths in an incremental constituency parse. The model jointly predicts next tokens and attachment decisions, using supervised silver/gold parses to update the stack tape and to softly bias attention via depth embeddings added to keys. Experiments on Dyck languages, BLLIP sentence-level language modeling, and a newly constructed parsed Wikipedia corpus (WIKITREES) show improved syntactic generalization and better sample efficiency relative to standard Transformers, while maintaining similar perplexity. The paper also reports modest gains when inserting Pushdown Layers into GPT-2 medium and finetuning on GLUE classification tasks.

Strengths: The core idea is novel and well motivated: introducing an explicit recursive-state mechanism into Transformer attention via a stack-inspired tape is a clean architectural contribution.; Pushdown Layers are presented as a drop-in replacement for self-attention rather than requiring a fully different decoding/output formalism, which improves practical appeal.; The method avoids expanding the output sequence with structural action tokens, addressing a key inefficiency of prior syntactic language models such as PLM/TG.; The paper evaluates across multiple settings: synthetic formal languages (Dyck), sentence-level syntactic generalization benchmarks (BLIMP, SG test suites), larger-scale parsed Wikipedia training, and downstream GLUE finetuning.; Results on syntactic generalization are strong and consistent, especially on recursive/center-embedding style phenomena and in low-data regimes.; The analysis section is useful: parsing quality is measured, and attention visualizations/case studies provide some interpretability for why the method helps.; Implementation details, pseudocode, hyperparameters, and code availability improve reproducibility.; The paper is generally clearly written, and figures such as the stack-tape update illustration (pages 1 and 4) help explain the mechanism.

Weaknesses: The approach depends on constituency parse supervision, often silver parses, which limits applicability to languages/domains without reliable parsers and makes gains partly contingent on external syntactic annotation quality.; The main comparisons are mostly against syntax-aware baselines from prior work, but some reported perplexities are not directly comparable due to tokenization differences, weakening claims about language-modeling quality.; Inference for marginal sentence probability requires beam search over parses, so the method is not as simple at test time as standard autoregressive LMs; computational cost and sensitivity to beam size are not deeply analyzed.; The memory overhead is substantially increased because keys become effectively 3D tensors indexed by stack-tape values; the paper acknowledges this but does not provide a thorough empirical runtime/memory benchmark.; Some downstream gains on GLUE are modest and mixed (e.g., SST5 slightly drops), so broader utility beyond syntactic generalization is only weakly established.; The method is evaluated only on English and synthetic languages, leaving multilingual robustness and transfer unclear.; Because training uses precomputed gold/silver stack tapes, there may be exposure mismatch between training and inference-time predicted structures; this issue is not deeply quantified.; The paper does not compare against simpler syntax-injection alternatives such as adding parser-derived features/embeddings without the full pushdown mechanism, so attribution of gains specifically to the stack dynamics is somewhat incomplete.

Soundness Issues: The methodology is generally sound, but the reliance on supervised attachment decisions and precomputed stack tapes means the model's gains may partly reflect access to external syntactic annotations rather than purely architectural superiority.; Approximate marginalization with beam search is necessary for evaluation on BLIMP/SG and parsing, yet the paper fixes beam size (300) without much ablation; conclusions may depend on this approximation quality.; For WIKITREES and GPT-2 finetuning, training data are automatically parsed by an external parser, so improvements may inherit parser biases/errors; the paper does not analyze robustness to noisy parses.; The claim of equal FLOPs to standard self-attention is plausible in asymptotic terms, but practical efficiency is clearly worse due to memory bandwidth and tensor shape changes; empirical profiling would strengthen this point.; Parsing evaluation compares against the parser used to annotate training data on BLLIP and against a filtered PTB subset due to unknown-word handling, which complicates interpretation of the parsing-quality claim.
