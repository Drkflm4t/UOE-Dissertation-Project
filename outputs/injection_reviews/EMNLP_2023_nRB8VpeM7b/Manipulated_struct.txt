Summary: The paper proposes Pushdown Layers, a drop-in replacement for Transformer self-attention that augments autoregressive language models with an explicit stack-like memory represented as a stack tape of token depths in an incremental constituency parse. The model jointly predicts next tokens and attachment decisions, uses the resulting depth information to softly modulate attention logits, and is trained with silver or gold constituency supervision. Empirically, the method improves generalization on Dyck languages, achieves stronger syntactic generalization than standard Transformer LMs and competitive prior syntax-aware baselines on BLIMP and SG test suites, shows substantially better sample efficiency on a newly constructed parsed Wikipedia corpus (WikiTrees), and yields modest gains on several GLUE tasks when inserted into GPT-2 medium. The paper also provides analysis suggesting that the induced structure helps attention avoid syntactic distractors and that the model can recover competitive parses.

Strengths: Novel architectural contribution: the stack-tape formulation is a clean and interesting way to inject recursive inductive bias into Transformer attention without expanding the output sequence with explicit transition tokens.; The method is conceptually elegant: parsing decisions and word predictions are made synchronously, avoiding some of the decoding complications of prior joint string-parse models.; Strong motivation grounded in the known difficulty of self-attention with recursive structure, and the proposed mechanism directly targets this limitation.; Empirical evaluation is broad: synthetic formal language experiments, sentence-level language modeling, larger-scale sample-efficiency experiments on WikiTrees, downstream finetuning on GLUE, and qualitative/quantitative analysis of parsing and attention behavior.; Results on syntactic generalization are compelling. On Dyck tasks the gains are large, and on BLIMP/SG the method clearly improves over the base Transformer while being competitive with or better than prior structured models.; The sample-efficiency result on WikiTrees is particularly interesting: the paper shows that syntax-aware inductive bias can substitute for substantial amounts of training data on syntactic benchmarks.; The approach preserves the standard LM output space and can be inserted into pretrained models, improving practical applicability relative to methods that require structural action vocabularies.; The analysis section is useful: competitive unlabeled parsing F1 and reduced attention to distractor nouns provide evidence that the mechanism is doing something structurally meaningful rather than merely adding parameters.; The paper is generally well written, with intuitive figures and pseudocode that make the mechanism understandable and reproducible.; Code availability and implementation details improve reproducibility.

Weaknesses: The approach depends on constituency parse supervision (often silver parses), which limits applicability to languages/domains without reliable parsers and makes some gains contingent on external syntactic annotation quality.; Several comparisons are not fully apples-to-apples. In Table 2, perplexities for prior structured models are marked as not directly comparable due to tokenization differences, and some reported differences on SG are noted as not significant.; The GLUE improvements are modest and somewhat inconsistent (e.g., one task slightly decreases), so the broader downstream impact beyond syntactic generalization remains limited.; Inference for marginalizing over parses relies on beam search with a fairly large beam, which may introduce nontrivial computational overhead and approximation error; runtime comparisons are not thoroughly quantified.; The memory overhead of Pushdown Layers is acknowledged to be higher due to 3D key tensors, but the paper does not provide a detailed empirical efficiency study (wall-clock time, peak memory, scaling with context length).; The experiments are limited to English and mostly syntax-centric evaluations, so it is unclear how well the method transfers to multilingual settings or to non-linguistic recursive domains.; The parsing evaluation compares against the parser used to generate silver annotations, but because training data are auto-parsed, it is difficult to disentangle genuine structural induction from imitation of the teacher parser.

Methodological Flaws: A key scientific limitation is that the central claim about improved recursive modeling is evaluated almost entirely in settings with explicit structural supervision. This makes it hard to attribute gains to the architectural inductive bias alone rather than to added parse supervision; stronger controls would include equally supervised baselines with comparable auxiliary signals but without stack-based attention modulation.; The paper claims the batched multiplication in Pushdown Layers leads to the same number of operations as standard self-attention, but this discussion is somewhat incomplete scientifically because practical complexity is not just FLOPs; the method materially increases memory from O(nd·ns + ns·d + nd·d) to O(nd·ns + ns·nd·d + nd·d), which can significantly affect throughput and scalability. The paper acknowledges this but does not empirically validate the tradeoff.; The use of argmax attachment decisions during inference (Eq. 5 / Algorithm 1) for stack updates may create exposure bias and error propagation in predicted parses, yet the paper does not analyze sensitivity to attachment errors or compare against stochastic/marginal alternatives.; For BLIMP and SG evaluations, p(x) is approximated via beam search over parses. Since these benchmarks compare sentence probabilities, approximation quality could affect conclusions; the paper fixes beam size at 300 but does not provide ablations showing stability with respect to beam size.; The WikiTrees corpus is automatically parsed using an external parser, so the large-scale experiments may partly measure consistency with parser-induced silver syntax rather than independent linguistic generalization. This does not invalidate the results, but it weakens causal claims about true syntactic competence.; The parsing analysis on PTB excludes sentences with unknown words (2335/2416 retained), which slightly complicates comparison to standard parsing results and may inflate reported performance relative to full-test-set evaluation.
