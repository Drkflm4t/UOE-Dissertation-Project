Summary: This paper introduces Pushdown Layers, a drop-in replacement for Transformer self-attention that augments language models with an explicit stack-like memory represented as a per-token stack tape of estimated parse depths. The model jointly predicts next tokens and attachment decisions that incrementally build unlabeled constituency structure, and uses depth embeddings to softly bias attention. Experiments on Dyck languages, BLLIP sentence-level language modeling, and a newly constructed parsed Wikipedia corpus (WIKITREES) show improved syntactic generalization and substantially better sample efficiency, while maintaining similar perplexity. The paper also reports modest gains when inserting Pushdown Layers into GPT-2 medium and finetuning on several GLUE tasks.

Strengths: Clear motivation: the paper targets a well-known weakness of self-attention for recursive structure and proposes an intuitively appropriate stack-based inductive bias.; Technically interesting architecture: the stack tape and attachment mechanism provide a relatively elegant way to inject recursive state into attention without changing the token output space.; Drop-in design: Pushdown Layers replace standard self-attention blocks and can be integrated into existing Transformer LMs, including GPT-2 finetuning.; Strong empirical breadth: evaluation spans synthetic formal languages, sentence-level syntactic generalization benchmarks (BLIMP and SG test suites), larger-scale Wikipedia language modeling, and downstream GLUE tasks.; Compelling gains on syntactic generalization and sample efficiency: the Dyck and WIKITREES results are especially persuasive, and BLLIP results are competitive with or better than prior structured LMs.; Useful analysis section: parsing quality, attention analyses on agreement tasks, and implementation details/code availability improve interpretability and reproducibility.; The method avoids some drawbacks of transition-token approaches, such as tripling sequence length and requiring more bespoke decoding over mixed word/structure action streams.

Weaknesses: The approach depends on constituency parse supervision, limiting applicability to languages/domains without high-quality parsers and making the inductive bias less broadly deployable.; Most empirical evidence is concentrated on English syntax and syntactic benchmarks; broader evaluation on multilingual settings or non-linguistic recursive tasks is absent.; The downstream gains on GLUE are modest and somewhat inconsistent (e.g., SST5 slightly drops), so practical benefits beyond syntax-sensitive evaluation remain limited.; Inference requires approximate marginalization with beam search over parses, which adds complexity and may reduce scalability compared with plain autoregressive LMs.; Memory overhead is substantially increased because keys become 3D tensors indexed by stack-tape values; the paper discusses this but does not provide a detailed runtime/memory benchmark.; Comparisons are not always perfectly apples-to-apples, especially perplexity comparisons to prior structured models due to tokenization differences.; The induced structure is unlabeled and binarized, which may limit expressivity relative to richer syntactic formalisms.

Soundness Issues: The core method is logically coherent: attachment decisions define stack updates, and stack-tape depths are then used to modulate attention in a principled way.; The experimental methodology is generally solid, with multiple benchmarks, ablations against a multitask baseline on Dyck, and comparisons to prior structured LMs.; A notable caveat is reliance on silver parses for large-scale training (WIKITREES), so some gains may partly reflect parser-induced biases rather than purely improved structural learning.; The paper approximates p(x) via beam search over parses; this is reasonable, but the quality/sensitivity of results to beam size and decoding approximation is not deeply analyzed.; The claim that Pushdown Layers are a drop-in replacement is mostly justified architecturally, but practical deployment costs are tempered by increased memory use and the need for structural supervision.; Some downstream conclusions should be interpreted cautiously because GLUE improvements are small and reported on only four tasks.
