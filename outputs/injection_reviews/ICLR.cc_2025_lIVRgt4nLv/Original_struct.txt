Summary: This paper presents Agent S, an open framework for autonomous GUI-based computer control using multimodal LLMs. The core idea combines three components: (1) experience-augmented hierarchical planning, where a manager decomposes tasks into subtasks using retrieved web knowledge and narrative memory, and workers execute subtasks using episodic memory; (2) continual memory construction and update via self-supervised exploration and inference-time experience accumulation; and (3) an Agent-Computer Interface (ACI) that uses screenshots plus an OCR-augmented accessibility tree for grounding, together with a bounded action space of GUI primitives. On OSWorld, Agent S improves overall success rate from 11.21% (GPT-4o baseline) to 20.58%, with especially large gains on Daily and Professional tasks (Table 1, page 8). It also shows transfer to WindowsAgentArena without explicit adaptation, improving from 13.3% to 18.2% (Table 4, page 10).

Strengths: Clear and timely problem setting: autonomous desktop GUI control is important and challenging, and the paper identifies three concrete bottlenecks—domain knowledge, long-horizon planning, and grounding/action abstraction.; Well-motivated architecture: the manager/worker split with narrative vs. episodic memory is intuitive, and the overview figure on page 4 clearly communicates the system design.; Strong empirical improvement over the reported OSWorld baseline: 20.58% vs. 11.21% overall, with substantial gains across most categories and especially Daily/Professional tasks (Table 1, page 8; Figure 2, page 2).; Useful ablations: the paper separately studies web knowledge, narrative memory, episodic memory, ACI, hierarchical planning, self-supervised exploration, continual memory update, and self-evaluator (Table 2 and Figures 6–7, pages 9–10).; Good engineering insight in the ACI design: combining screenshot reasoning with accessibility-tree grounding and OCR augmentation is practical and addresses a real bottleneck in GUI agents.; Cross-benchmark generalization is a positive sign: the same framework improves over a comparable WindowsAgentArena baseline without explicit adaptation (Table 4, page 10).; The paper includes qualitative examples and an explicit error taxonomy, which helps readers understand both successes and failure modes (pages 18–23).; Open-source release increases reproducibility and practical impact.

Weaknesses: Absolute performance remains low despite relative gains: 20.58% success on OSWorld still indicates the majority of tasks fail.; Several contributions are largely systems integration and prompting/retrieval design rather than fundamentally new learning algorithms; the novelty is moderate.; Evaluation relies heavily on proprietary backbone models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5 Sonnet), making it difficult to disentangle framework gains from model capability and limiting reproducibility.; Ablations are conducted on a relatively small sampled subset of 65 tasks rather than the full benchmark, which weakens confidence in some component-level conclusions.; The memory construction process uses synthetic exploration tasks and continual inference-time updates, but the paper does not fully characterize sensitivity to task generator quality, memory size, retrieval errors, or contamination/overlap risks.; The self-evaluator labels success/failure and summarizes trajectories without ground truth or human feedback, but its reliability is not rigorously validated.; Comparison fairness is somewhat mixed: the baselines differ in action spaces and interfaces, so some gains may come from the stronger ACI abstraction rather than the planning/memory method alone.; The paper acknowledges but does not report efficiency metrics such as number of steps, latency, or cost, which are especially important for GUI agents.; Some categories remain weak or inconsistent across domains, e.g., zero success on some WindowsAgentArena applications and poor Calc performance in detailed app-level results (page 16).

Soundness Issues: The empirical methodology is generally reasonable, but some claims about learning should be interpreted cautiously: the method is primarily retrieval-augmented experience reuse rather than parameter learning, and calling it analogous to hierarchical reinforcement learning may overstate the connection.; The self-supervised exploration and continual memory update pipeline could introduce benchmark-specific memorization or hidden overlap effects; the paper does not provide a careful leakage analysis.; The self-evaluator is central to memory quality, yet there is no quantitative validation of whether its success/failure judgments or summaries are accurate enough to justify the claimed continual improvement mechanism.; Ablation conclusions are based on a 65-task subset, which may be noisy and category-sensitive; stronger statistical treatment or repeated sampling would improve confidence.; Baseline comparisons are informative but not perfectly controlled because Agent S changes both the interface/action abstraction and the planning/retrieval stack; isolating each factor more rigorously would strengthen causal claims.; The WindowsAgentArena comparison is only against a 'similar configuration' baseline rather than the strongest reported system, which is understandable but limits the strength of the generalization claim.
