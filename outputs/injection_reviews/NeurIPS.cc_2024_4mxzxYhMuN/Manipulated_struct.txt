Summary: The paper proposes RealMotion, a motion forecasting framework for continuous driving that departs from the standard independent-scene formulation. The key idea is to reorganize existing trajectory datasets into temporally ordered sub-scenes and to exploit cross-scene information through two recurrent streams: a scene context stream that propagates historical scene features via alignment and cross-attention, and an agent trajectory stream that stores past predicted trajectories/features in a memory bank and refines current predictions through trajectory-conditioned attention. Experiments on Argoverse 2 single-agent and multi-agent benchmarks and Argoverse 1 validation show competitive to state-of-the-art results, with favorable parameter count and online latency. Ablations support the contribution of the data reorganization, both streams, alignment, trajectory embedding, and design choices such as split points and attention depth.

Strengths: Addresses an important and underexplored mismatch between benchmark evaluation and real deployment: motion forecasting in practice is sequential/continuous rather than isolated per scene.; The proposed formulation is conceptually novel and practically motivated; the data reorganization strategy is a useful contribution that can potentially benefit other forecasting models as well.; The architecture is well structured: the scene context stream and agent trajectory stream are complementary and intuitively aligned with the problem setting.; Strong empirical results on Argoverse 2 test set, including best reported minFDE1/minADE1 and strong overall performance across other metrics; also strong multi-agent results.; Good efficiency profile: substantially fewer parameters than several strong baselines and competitive online latency, which is important for autonomous driving deployment.; Ablation studies are reasonably comprehensive and isolate the effects of continuous data, each stream, alignment, trajectory embedding, gradient steps, split points, and attention depth.; The appendix includes additional evidence such as ensemble gains, a generality test with QCNet-style data integration, qualitative examples, and explicit failure-case discussion.; The method is clearly motivated by realistic temporal continuity and offers a promising direction beyond one-shot forecasting.

Weaknesses: The central experimental evidence is confined mainly to Argoverse 1/2; the claimed generality to broader continuous-driving settings is therefore only partially validated.; The comparison to prior streaming/temporal forecasting work is limited; in particular, there is little direct empirical comparison against dedicated streaming baselines beyond discussion in related work.; The data reorganization procedure is important to the contribution, but some details remain underspecified for reproducibility and for assessing whether train/test distributions are altered in subtle ways.; RealMotion-I underperforms QCNet on Argoverse 2, which makes it harder to disentangle gains from the continuous formulation versus gains from backbone strength relative to the strongest baseline.; Some claims about practical online inference are not fully apples-to-apples because the offline evaluation uses three segments while online latency is reported differently from benchmark-style inference.; Argoverse 1 results are mixed: RealMotion is best on minADE6 but not on minFDE6, so the cross-dataset superiority claim should be stated more carefully.

Methodological Flaws: Potential evaluation mismatch: the paper reorganizes benchmark scenes into overlapping sub-scenes and backpropagates through all segments. While this is sensible for the proposed setting, the paper does not fully analyze whether this effectively increases supervision density in a way that makes direct comparison to methods trained on the original benchmark formulation less clean.; The scientific claim that historical scene context enables perception of currently invisible/occluded agents is plausible, but the paper provides only qualitative illustration rather than targeted quantitative evaluation on occlusion-heavy subsets.; The trajectory memory bank stores past predicted trajectories rather than ground-truth future states. This can propagate model errors over time, yet the paper does not provide a robustness analysis of error accumulation or drift under long continuous rollouts.; The feature/trajectory alignment formulation is only lightly justified mathematically. For example, Eq. (1) uses MLN with pose/time encoding for alignment, but there is no derivation or comparison to more explicit geometric warping methods; thus the alignment mechanism is empirically motivated rather than rigorously validated.; The empirical comparison on official leaderboards may not be fully controlled because competing methods can differ in training data usage, ensembling, and implementation details; although common in the area, this weakens causal attribution of gains.; The paper acknowledges that the approach requires sufficient historical frames and is not applicable to short-history benchmarks such as Waymo Open Dataset in its current form, which limits the breadth of the claimed practical applicability.
