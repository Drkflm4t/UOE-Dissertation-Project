Summary: This paper studies motion forecasting under a more realistic continuous-driving setting, arguing that standard benchmarks and methods treat each scene independently and therefore ignore temporal relationships across successive forecasting moments. The authors propose RealMotion, which reorganizes benchmark scenes into short continuous sub-scene sequences and introduces two recurrent scene-level streams: a scene context stream that carries forward historical scene features via alignment and cross-attention, and an agent trajectory stream that stores past predicted trajectories/features in a memory bank and refines current predictions through trajectory-conditioned attention. Built on a relatively simple encoder-decoder backbone, the method is evaluated on Argoverse 1 and 2 for single-agent and multi-agent forecasting. Reported results show strong gains over prior work on Argoverse 2 test benchmarks and consistent ablation improvements from the proposed data reorganization and both temporal streams, while maintaining competitive online inference latency.

Strengths: Addresses an important and underexplored mismatch between benchmark evaluation and real deployment: forecasting is naturally repeated over time, not performed on isolated snapshots.; The core idea is intuitive and practically motivated: exploit temporal continuity both at the scene-context level and at the trajectory-prediction level.; Method design is reasonably clean and modular. The two-stream decomposition (context accumulation plus trajectory relaying) is easy to understand and appears broadly compatible with existing forecasting architectures.; Strong empirical results are reported on Argoverse 2 single-agent and multi-agent leaderboards, with especially notable gains on minFDE1/minADE1 and competitive results on other metrics.; Ablation studies are fairly comprehensive: they isolate the effects of continuous data reorganization, each stream, alignment modules, trajectory embedding, split points, gradient steps, and cross-attention depth.; Efficiency analysis is a plus: the model is relatively small and the reported online latency is competitive, which supports the practical motivation.; The appendix includes a limited generality check by applying the data reorganization idea to QCNet, suggesting that at least part of the contribution is not backbone-specific.; The paper openly discusses limitations and failure cases, including dependence on sufficiently long history and difficulties with complex topology or subjective behaviors.

Weaknesses: A substantial part of the gain may come from the proposed data reorganization / evaluation setting rather than the model itself; disentangling benchmark reshaping from architectural contribution is not fully convincing.; The comparison to prior methods is not entirely apples-to-apples because most baselines were not adapted to the same continuous-driving setup, while RealMotion is explicitly designed around reorganized sequential inputs.; The independent baseline RealMotion-I is weaker than top methods such as QCNet on several metrics, suggesting that the backbone itself is not especially strong; this makes it harder to assess whether the main contribution is a new forecasting model or a new problem framing plus data transformation.; Methodological details around the data reorganization are somewhat underspecified for reproducibility and fairness: exact sequence construction choices, overlap statistics, train/val/test handling, and whether reorganized samples induce leakage-like correlations are not deeply analyzed.; The trajectory stream relies on past predictions stored in memory, which may propagate errors over time; while intuitively plausible, the paper does not deeply analyze robustness to erroneous historical predictions or long-horizon drift.; The claimed practical realism is still limited because experiments are conducted on transformed offline benchmarks rather than a true streaming benchmark or closed-loop autonomous driving system.; Argoverse 1 results are mixed: RealMotion improves minADE6 but is not best on minFDE6, so the evidence of broad superiority is stronger on Argoverse 2 than universally.; The paper does not compare directly against the most relevant prior streaming/temporal forecasting work in a controlled setting, despite citing it.

Soundness Issues: The central premise is sound, but the empirical validation conflates two factors: changing the data structure into sequential sub-scenes and introducing temporal modules. Although ablations help, comparisons against external baselines remain potentially unfair unless those baselines are retrained/evaluated under the same reorganized setting.; The data reorganization procedure may create highly correlated sub-scenes from the same original sample. This is not necessarily invalid, but the paper does not sufficiently discuss whether this changes the effective training distribution or inflates gains relative to standard benchmark protocols.; The memory-bank refinement mechanism uses previous predictions rather than ground truth, which is realistic, but there is limited analysis of stability under compounding prediction errors across long sequences.; The loss design is simple and acceptable, but equal weighting of regression, classification, and refinement losses appears heuristic and is not justified empirically.; The efficiency comparison mixes online and offline settings and notes that RealMotion offline latency effectively includes three iterations; this is informative, but the practical comparison could be clearer and more standardized.; Generality evidence is limited: only the data reorganization is tested with QCNet, while the full RealMotion streams are not integrated due to memory constraints, so claims of generic applicability should be interpreted cautiously.
