Summary: The paper proposes RealMotion, a motion forecasting framework for continuous driving that departs from the standard assumption of independently forecasting each scene. The core idea is to reorganize existing benchmark scenes into temporally ordered sub-scene sequences and to exploit cross-scene information via two recurrent streams: (1) a scene context stream that propagates historical scene features through cross-attention after alignment, and (2) an agent trajectory stream that stores prior predicted trajectories/features in a memory bank and refines current predictions via trajectory-conditioned attention. The method is evaluated primarily on Argoverse 2 and Argoverse 1, with reported gains over prior methods on several leaderboard metrics and favorable latency/parameter counts relative to selected baselines. Ablations suggest that both the data reorganization and the two temporal streams contribute to performance improvements.

Strengths: Addresses a practically relevant but underexplored setting: continuous/streaming motion forecasting rather than isolated per-scene prediction.; The proposed decomposition into a scene-context stream and a trajectory-memory stream is intuitive and reasonably well motivated.; Strong empirical results are reported on Argoverse 2 single-agent and multi-agent leaderboards, including improvements over QCNet on several key metrics.; Ablation studies cover major components, including data reorganization, each stream, alignment modules, trajectory embedding, split points, and cross-attention depth.; The method appears computationally efficient relative to some strong baselines, with a small parameter count and competitive online latency.; The paper is clearly structured, and figures/tables help communicate the continuous-driving motivation and architecture.

Weaknesses: The central experimental setting relies on a synthetic data reorganization of standard benchmarks rather than a native continuous-driving benchmark, so the claimed real-world relevance is only partially validated.; Comparison fairness is unclear because RealMotion changes both the model and the training/evaluation data structure; many baseline numbers come from official leaderboards and may not be directly comparable under identical preprocessing and protocol.; The independent baseline RealMotion-I is substantially weaker than QCNet, suggesting the base architecture is not itself competitive; much of the gain may come from the altered data setup rather than the proposed temporal modeling alone.; The paper lacks rigorous analysis of error accumulation and robustness in the trajectory memory stream, where past predictions are recursively reused and could propagate mistakes.; Methodological details are underspecified in places: memory bank size n, retrieval policy, whether memory is per-agent identity or per focal-agent sequence, handling of agent appearance/disappearance, and exact online inference protocol are not fully clarified.; The Argoverse 1 comparison is on validation rather than test/leaderboard, and the reported metrics are mixed in strength (e.g., best minADE6 but not best minFDE6), so the cross-dataset SOTA claim is somewhat overstated.; The efficiency comparison is limited and not fully apples-to-apples: RealMotion offline latency is effectively multiplied by the number of segments, while competing methods may not be measured under equivalent preprocessing or hardware/software settings.; There are several presentation issues and minor notation inconsistencies/typos, which reduce confidence in the technical precision.

Methodological Flaws: A key scientific validity concern is that the paper claims to solve motion forecasting in a more realistic continuous-driving setting, but the training/evaluation setup is created by retrospectively splitting independent benchmark scenes into overlapping sub-scenes. This does not fully reproduce true streaming conditions, because all sub-scenes originate from the same annotated sample and may induce strong temporal redundancy and leakage-like advantages relative to standard one-shot baselines.; The comparison to prior leaderboard methods is not methodologically clean. RealMotion uses a reorganized sequential input structure and, for offline evaluation, effectively performs multiple forecasting passes per original sample while only the final prediction is scored. This gives access to additional intermediate supervision/context not available to standard baselines in their reported leaderboard numbers.; The paper does not provide a controlled reimplementation of strong baselines under the same continuous-data protocol, except a limited QCNet integration in the appendix. Therefore it is difficult to isolate whether gains come from the proposed architecture or simply from the extra sequentialized training signal.; Equation (1) / alignment description is mathematically vague and appears incomplete in notation (parentheses mismatch in the text, unclear exact transform from historical local coordinates to current local coordinates). More importantly, using MLN with relative pose encoding is not an explicit geometric alignment of scene elements, so the claim of 'projecting' historical scene features into the current coordinate system is stronger than what is actually implemented.; The trajectory-memory refinement in Eq. (4) uses predicted trajectories from prior steps as memory without a principled uncertainty-aware mechanism or filtering. This can introduce confirmation bias and error propagation, yet the paper provides no robustness analysis under inaccurate past predictions.; The loss design uses equal weights for regression, classification, and refinement losses without justification or sensitivity analysis. Given the added refinement stage, this choice may materially affect results.; Empirical validity for the multi-agent setting is limited: the method is not specifically designed for joint forecasting, yet claims superiority based on a small comparison table against few methods, without deeper analysis of interaction consistency or scalability.; The paper's claim that the data reorganization comes at 'no extra cost' is misleading. While it may reuse existing annotations, it increases training/inference passes per original scene and changes the effective supervision budget and latency accounting.
