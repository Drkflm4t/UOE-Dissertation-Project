Summary: This paper proposes CoT-Influx, a plug-and-play prompting method to improve LLM mathematical reasoning by fitting more useful few-shot chain-of-thought examples into a fixed context window. The method uses a coarse-to-fine pruner: a shot-level pruner selects helpful CoT exemplars and a token-level pruner removes redundant tokens while preserving reasoning-critical structure and numbers. Because pruning decisions are discrete and external to the frozen target LLM, the pruner is trained with REINFORCE using a multi-objective reward combining LLM loss, answer correctness, and token-budget penalties. The authors also construct MRD3, a GPT-4-generated/evolved math reasoning dataset with difficulty annotations for training the pruner. Experiments on LLaMA2-7B/13B/70B across GSM8K, AddSub, MultiArith, SVAMP, and SingleEq show consistent gains over standard few-shot CoT, retrieval baselines, and prompt compression baselines, with the strongest headline result being LLaMA2-70B + CoT-Influx outperforming GPT-3.5 on GSM8K in their setting.

Strengths: Clear and practically motivated idea: exploit redundancy at both exemplar and token levels to increase effective few-shot CoT density without modifying the target LLM.; Well-designed coarse-to-fine architecture; the shot-pruning then token-pruning decomposition is intuitive and supported by ablations.; Strong empirical evaluation across multiple LLaMA2 scales and five math benchmarks, with consistent improvements over standard prompting baselines.; Useful comparisons against both retrieval methods and prompt compression methods; the paper convincingly shows generic compression methods often damage math-relevant structure/tokens.; Good ablation studies on MRD3, reward components, and pruning stages; these help justify the main design choices.; Practical deployment appeal: the method is black-box with respect to the target LLM and adds little inference overhead according to the reported measurements.; The appendix includes informative qualitative case studies and analyses, including examples of how other compression methods break prompt structure and how CoT-Influx preserves it better.

Weaknesses: The method depends heavily on GPT-4-generated/evolved data (MRD3) and GPT-4-based difficulty annotations, which raises concerns about cost, reproducibility, and possible benchmark contamination or hidden teacher advantages.; Evaluation is concentrated on arithmetic/math word problems; claims about broader reasoning improvement are not established.; The training/evaluation pipeline includes several heuristic components (difficulty filtering, manual 8-shot CoT appended during training, rule-based prompt reconstruction) that complicate attribution of gains solely to the learned pruner.; Comparisons to retrieval baselines may not be fully optimized or equally strong; the paper mainly uses TopK/BM25/random and does not compare against stronger learned retrievers tailored to math or more recent ICL selection methods in the main tables.; The RL formulation is somewhat brittle and high-variance; although stabilization tricks are introduced, the paper does not report variance across random seeds or confidence intervals.; The token-pruning stage appears to rely on BERT embeddings truncated/padded to 512 tokens per exemplar, which may limit handling of longer CoTs and may discard useful information.; Some claims are stronger than the evidence supports, e.g., 'no additional inference costs' is overstated since there is still some added latency/memory from the external module, even if small.; The paper does not deeply analyze failure cases or when pruning harms performance, beyond noting an optimal number of shots exists.

Soundness Issues: The methodology is generally reasonable, but the use of GPT-4 to generate and evolve training exemplars plus annotate difficulty introduces a potential confound: improvements may partly stem from high-quality synthetic demonstrations rather than the pruning method itself.; The reward mixes token-level compression, LLM loss, and exact-match correctness, but the paper does not provide enough detail on reward scaling sensitivity or variance beyond limited appendix ablations; RL stability remains a concern.; Appending manually designed 8-shot CoTs during training but evaluating CoT-Influx as a standalone prompting improvement may blur what the pruner itself learns versus what is inherited from the manual prompt scaffold.; Rule-based prompt reconstruction after pruning is an important post-processing step; because it repairs structural tokens like 'Q:', 'A:', and 'The answer is', the final performance is not purely due to learned token selection.; The comparison to context extension in the appendix is narrow (mainly PI on LLaMA2-7B) and may not justify broader claims against long-context methods.; Results are reported as point estimates without statistical uncertainty, making it hard to assess robustness of relatively modest gains such as ~1-4.5% absolute on some settings.
