Summary: The paper proposes CoT-Influx, a plug-and-play prompting module for improving LLM mathematical reasoning by increasing the number of effective few-shot chain-of-thought examples within a fixed context window. The method uses a coarse-to-fine pruner: a shot-level selector chooses useful CoT exemplars from a larger retrieved pool, and a token-level pruner removes allegedly redundant tokens from retained exemplars. Because pruning decisions are discrete and external to the target LLM, the pruner is trained with REINFORCE using a reward that combines LLM loss, answer correctness, and a token-budget term. The paper also introduces MRD3, a GPT-4-generated/evolved math CoT dataset with difficulty annotations, used to train the pruner. Experiments on LLaMA2-7B/13B/70B across GSM8K, AddSub, MultiArith, SVAMP, and SingleEq show gains over standard few-shot CoT, retrieval baselines, and prompt compression baselines, with the strongest headline result being 59.6 EM on GSM8K for LLaMA2-70B + CoT-Influx.

Strengths: Clear and practically motivated problem: improving few-shot CoT under fixed context budgets without modifying or fine-tuning the target LLM.; The coarse-to-fine design is intuitive and well aligned with the stated observations from the pilot study: example selection matters and token redundancy exists.; Broad empirical evaluation across multiple LLaMA2 scales and five math benchmarks, with consistent improvements over the paper's chosen prompting baselines.; Useful ablations isolate contributions of MRD3, shot pruning, token pruning, and reward components.; The method is black-box with respect to the target LLM and appears lightweight at inference relative to context-extension approaches.; The appendix includes qualitative case studies showing why generic compression methods may damage math prompts by removing structural or numerical tokens.

Weaknesses: The empirical gains, while consistent, are modest on the main benchmark relative to the complexity of the pipeline; e.g., GSM8K gains over standard few-shot CoT are about 2.1-4.6 points depending on model.; The method relies heavily on GPT-4 for dataset generation, evolution, difficulty scoring, and some comparisons, making the overall contribution less self-contained and potentially expensive or hard to reproduce exactly.; The evaluation is restricted mostly to math word problems and LLaMA2-family models; claims about broader reasoning or general plug-and-play applicability are not convincingly established.; Several comparisons are not fully balanced: CoT-Influx uses a learned pruner trained specifically for the target model/task, whereas retrieval and compression baselines are largely off-the-shelf and not similarly adapted.; The paper uses manually appended 8-shot CoTs and rule-based prompt reconstruction during inference/training, which weakens the claim that the learned pruner alone is responsible for the gains.; Presentation has some inconsistencies and imprecision in notation and algorithm description, making the method harder to verify rigorously.

Methodological Flaws: The optimization objective in Eq. (2) is ill-posed/inconsistent: it simultaneously writes a minimization of L_LLM and a maximization of R_Acc over the same variable D subset, but not as a single coherent scalarized objective. The actual training objective is only later introduced via Eq. (5), so the formal problem statement does not faithfully match the implemented optimization.; The reward definition in Eq. (5) is scientifically questionable. The token-budget factor [t/T]^w is described as penalizing both overly short prompts (w<0) and over-budget prompts (w>0), but Table 14 sets w to '(-1,1)', which is not a single scalar as required by Eq. (5). This suggests either undocumented piecewise logic or a mismatch between paper and implementation.; The REINFORCE gradient in Eq. (6) omits standard variance-reduction details and appears incomplete as written; moreover, the paper does not clearly specify whether the reward is baseline-subtracted, how credit is assigned between shot and token actions, or how the categorical sampling is parameterized given the earlier use of sigmoid outputs.; There is a substantial confound from rule-based prompt reconstruction (Appendix C.5). Since pruned outputs are post-processed to restore key structural tokens such as 'Q:', 'A:', 'Let’s think step by step', and 'The answer is', the reported performance is not purely due to learned token pruning. This especially weakens comparisons against compression baselines that are not given analogous reconstruction.; Another confound is the manual 8-shot CoT appended during training/inference for stabilization (Algorithm 1 line 14 and Sec. 4.4). This means the final prompt is not solely the pruned retrieved/evolved context, and part of the gain may come from always including a strong handcrafted scaffold.; The training/evaluation split may induce leakage-like concerns. MRD3 is built partly from GSM8K training data and evolved variants, then used both as prompt candidate pool and pruner-training source, while evaluation includes GSM8K validation. This is not direct label leakage, but the heavy use of GPT-4-evolved variants from the same benchmark family may over-specialize the method to GSM8K-style distributions.; The comparison to larger LLMs in Table 4 is not apples-to-apples. Different models come from different papers, prompting setups, decoding strategies, and possibly evaluation protocols. Using this table to claim superiority over GPT-3.5/PaLM/Minerva is therefore weaker than implied.; The claim of 'no additional inference costs' is overstated. Even if the final prompt fits the original context window, CoT-Influx still requires running BERT and the pruner at inference time; Table 8 indeed reports extra latency and memory.; The pilot study and main experiments do not adequately disentangle whether gains come from more examples, better example selection, token pruning, the appended manual CoTs, or reconstruction heuristics. The ablations help, but they do not fully isolate these interacting components under matched token budgets.; The method is trained with batch size 1 and very limited reporting of variance or seed sensitivity. No confidence intervals or multi-run statistics are provided, which is problematic given the stochasticity of REINFORCE and decoding.
