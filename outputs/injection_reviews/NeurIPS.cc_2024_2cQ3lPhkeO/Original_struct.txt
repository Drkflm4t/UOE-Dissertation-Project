Summary: This paper studies reward overoptimization in offline RLHF and argues that it arises from distributional shift plus uncertainty in learned preference rewards. The authors propose a theoretical maximin objective that selects a policy against an adversarial reward model minimizing the sum of Bradley-Terry MLE loss and a reward penalty term. They show finite-sample guarantees under a partial coverage assumption with general function approximation. They then derive an equivalent minimax formulation whose practical instantiation, Regularized Preference Optimization (RPO), reduces to a simple combination of DPO-style preference loss and an imitation/SFT loss. Empirically, on Zephyr beta and gemma training setups, RPO improves over DPO on held-out in-distribution pairwise evaluations, MT-Bench, AlpacaEval 2.0, and some math/reasoning/coding benchmarks, while also mitigating the drop in log-probability on chosen responses during training.

Strengths: Addresses an important and practically relevant RLHF failure mode: reward overoptimization under imperfect preference learning.; Provides a clear conceptual contribution linking overoptimization mitigation to an adversarial/pessimistic objective and interpreting SFT loss as an implicit adversarial regularizer.; The theoretical development is nontrivial: the paper gives a maximin formulation, proves an equivalence to a minimax objective under regularity assumptions, and derives finite-sample suboptimality bounds under partial coverage.; Works under general function approximation rather than only linear reward classes, which is a meaningful theoretical generalization.; The practical algorithm is simple and easy to implement: RPO is just DPO plus an SFT/imitation term, making the method accessible and low-overhead.; Empirical results are reasonably broad: two model families/datasets, in-distribution pairwise evaluation, MT-Bench, AlpacaEval 2.0, and additional GSM8K/ARC/MBPP results.; The paper includes intuitive illustrations, especially the toy example on page 3 explaining why DPO can allocate probability mass to undesirable uncovered responses while RPO avoids this.

Weaknesses: The theoretical guarantees are for the idealized optimization problems over policy/reward classes, but the practical experiments optimize large neural policies with SGD; the gap between theory and actual training dynamics is substantial and not tightly bridged.; The equivalence and guarantees rely on regularity assumptions on the reward class (compactness, convex-like structure) that may not hold for realistic neural parameterizations used in LLM alignment.; The empirical gains over DPO, while consistent, appear modest on some benchmark metrics, and the paper does not deeply analyze sensitivity to the regularization coefficient η or robustness across seeds.; Evaluation relies heavily on GPT-4-as-judge for pairwise comparisons and benchmark scoring, which introduces known evaluator bias and noise.; The in-distribution pairwise evaluation uses only 150 filtered examples, which is fairly small for strong empirical claims.; The practical choice of baseline policy for the SFT term is justified only loosely in experiments; more ablations on different baseline distributions would strengthen the paper.; The paper argues that decreasing log-probability on chosen responses indicates overoptimization, but this proxy is suggestive rather than definitive and could benefit from stronger causal validation.

Soundness Issues: The main theorem depends on a partial coverage coefficient relative to the target policy and baseline policy; in realistic LLM settings this assumption may be hard to verify and may fail in important regimes, limiting the practical force of the guarantee.; The minimax/maximin equivalence requires convex-like reward classes and uniqueness properties induced by KL regularization; these assumptions are mathematically plausible in abstract spaces but not obviously satisfied by neural reward/policy parameterizations.; The practical RPO objective in Eq. (4.5) is motivated by the theoretical derivation, but the paper does not provide optimization-error or approximation-error analysis for finite-capacity parameterized policies, so the end-to-end guarantee for actual LLM training is incomplete.; Generalization to new prompt distributions is reduced to a bounded density-ratio assumption; the PCA plots on page 12 are only heuristic evidence and do not establish the required coverage condition.; Empirical comparisons appear limited mainly to DPO-style baselines; stronger comparisons to other overoptimization-mitigation methods would better support claims of practical superiority.
