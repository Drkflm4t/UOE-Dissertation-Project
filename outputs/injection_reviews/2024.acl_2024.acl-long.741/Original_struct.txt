Summary: This paper studies user-side privacy protection for online self-disclosures. It introduces a 19-category taxonomy of self-disclosure, a Reddit corpus of 2.4K posts with 4.8K annotated disclosure spans, and models for span-level disclosure detection, disclosure abstraction, and disclosure-importance rating. The detection model achieves about 65.7 partial span F1 on the multi-class task, and the authors complement benchmark evaluation with a 21-participant Reddit user study in which most participants viewed the tool positively. Motivated by user feedback, the paper proposes span-level self-disclosure abstraction: rewriting a detected disclosure into a less specific but still useful form. Fine-tuned Llama-2 models generate diverse abstractions with moderate privacy gains and relatively high utility in human evaluation. The paper also introduces an importance-rating task to help decide which disclosures should be removed, abstracted, or kept, with a fine-tuned model reaching about 80% accuracy, comparable to GPT-3.5.

Strengths: Addresses an important and underexplored problem: protecting user privacy at the point of self-disclosure rather than only downstream anonymization or PII masking.; Strong task formulation: span-level detection is more actionable than sentence/post classification, and span-level abstraction is a novel, practically motivated extension.; Creates a useful taxonomy with broad coverage (19 categories) spanning both demographic attributes and personal experiences; Table 1 on page 2 clearly demonstrates the breadth.; Includes a substantial annotation effort with 2.4K Reddit posts and 4.8K annotated spans, plus additional experiments on ShareGPT-style conversations for generalization.; Human-centered methodology is a major strength: the user study with Reddit users directly informs the abstraction and importance-rating tasks, making the work more compelling than a purely benchmark-driven paper.; Empirical evaluation is reasonably comprehensive, covering fine-tuned encoder baselines, prompted GPT-4 comparisons, automatic metrics, and multiple human evaluations.; The comparison between sentence paraphrasing, sentence abstraction, and span abstraction (Table 3 on page 5 and Table 5 on page 6) helps justify the proposed task design.; Ethics and release considerations are taken seriously, with restricted access and explicit discussion of privacy risks and annotation safeguards.

Weaknesses: Detection performance, while promising, is still moderate for a deployment-oriented privacy tool; partial span F1 around 65% leaves substantial room for misses and false positives.; Inter-annotator agreement is only moderate/fair for several core tasks: 0.54 alpha for disclosure annotation, 0.37-0.41 for abstraction ranking, and 0.29 for importance rating, which raises concerns about task definition clarity and label reliability.; The user study is small (21 participants) and qualitative; positive reception is encouraging but not sufficient to establish robust real-world effectiveness or behavioral impact.; Abstraction training data is generated automatically by GPT-3.5/GPT-4 rather than by humans, so the abstraction models may inherit stylistic biases or errors from teacher models.; Evaluation of abstraction relies heavily on subjective human judgments with modest agreement, and the reported privacy improvement is only moderate (around 3.2-3.4/5 in Figure 2 on page 7).; The paper does not evaluate the full end-to-end pipeline under realistic error propagation from detection to abstraction and importance rating.; Some categories are extremely sparse (e.g., name and contact in Table 1 on page 2), forcing hybrid heuristics rather than a unified modeling approach.; The restricted release policy is understandable ethically, but it may limit reproducibility and broader community validation.

Soundness Issues: The methodology is generally sound, but the moderate annotation agreement suggests the underlying constructs are subjective and somewhat unstable; conclusions should therefore be framed as exploratory rather than definitive.; The main detection metric, partial span F1, is reasonable for this task, but it is forgiving on boundaries; stronger evidence for practical utility would come from end-to-end user-facing error analysis and calibration of false negatives versus false positives.; Importance-rating accuracy is computed by counting a prediction as correct if it matches any of the three annotators, which is lenient and may overstate performance given the low agreement reported on pages 7-8.; Abstraction quality claims depend on LLM-generated references and subjective ratings rather than objective privacy-risk reduction measures; the paper shows usefulness but not rigorous privacy guarantees.; The user study supports practical relevance, but because participants reviewed model outputs on selected posts and hypothetical usage scenarios, it does not yet demonstrate that the system actually reduces harmful disclosures in deployment.
