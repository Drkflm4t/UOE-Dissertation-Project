Summary: The paper addresses user-side privacy risks in online self-disclosure by introducing a broad-coverage, span-level framework with three components: (1) a new taxonomy of 19 self-disclosure categories and a manually annotated Reddit corpus of 2.4K posts / 4.8K spans; (2) fine-grained self-disclosure detection models that identify disclosure spans at word level, achieving about 65.7 partial span F1 with DeBERTaV3-large; and (3) a novel self-disclosure abstraction task that rewrites sensitive spans into less specific alternatives while preserving utility, along with an importance-rating task to help decide which disclosures should be abstracted. The paper also includes an HCI interview study with 21 Reddit users, where 82% of participants viewed the tool positively. Overall, the work is ambitious, timely, and meaningfully connects NLP modeling with privacy and human-centered evaluation.

Strengths: Addresses an important and underexplored problem: protecting user-side privacy in voluntary online self-disclosures rather than only detecting classic PII.; Introduces a useful and relatively comprehensive taxonomy of 19 disclosure categories spanning both demographic attributes and personal experiences.; Creates a substantial annotated resource with span-level labels, which is more fine-grained and practically actionable than sentence/document-level classification.; Combines NLP modeling with a real user study, which substantially strengthens the practical relevance of the work.; The detection setup is thoughtfully designed and evaluated across multiple models, granular metrics, and even an out-of-domain ShareGPT setting.; The abstraction task is novel and well motivated by user feedback; the paper clearly distinguishes span abstraction from paraphrasing and sentence-level abstraction.; Human evaluation for abstraction considers multiple dimensions (privacy increase, utility preservation, diversity, coherence), which is appropriate for this task.; The importance-rating task is a useful addition that directly responds to user concerns about over-highlighting and selective intervention.; The paper includes strong ethical consideration, IRB approval, restricted release plans, and discussion of misuse risks.; The overall paper is well organized, with clear examples, informative tables/figures, and strong end-to-end motivation from problem definition to deployment considerations.

Weaknesses: Inter-annotator agreement is only moderate/fair for several tasks (e.g., 0.54 overall for detection annotation, 0.29 for importance rating, and modest agreement in abstraction evaluation), which indicates substantial subjectivity in the problem formulation.; The empirical gains for some components are moderate rather than decisive; for example, detection partial F1 around 65 and abstraction privacy increase around 3.2/5 suggest the problem remains challenging.; The user study is valuable but relatively small (21 participants) and limited to Reddit users, which may constrain generalizability to broader populations and platforms.; Some categories are very sparse (e.g., name/contact), requiring hybrid handling rather than unified modeling, which exposes limitations of the dataset coverage.; For abstraction and importance rating, the train/dev/test splits appear fairly small, and several results rely heavily on human evaluation rather than robust automatic benchmarks.; Pipeline evaluation is missing: detection, importance rating, and abstraction are evaluated mostly separately, so end-to-end utility in realistic deployment remains uncertain.

Methodological Flaws: The paper uses partial span F1 with a relatively forgiving overlap criterion (>50% of the longer span), which is reasonable for noisy span boundaries but may overstate practical extraction quality compared with exact-span evaluation; exact span F1 is reported only in the appendix and is notably lower.; Several core evaluations rely on subjective human judgments with only fair agreement, especially for importance rating and some abstraction dimensions. This does not invalidate the work, but it weakens the strength of claims about precise model superiority.; The importance-rating evaluation counts a prediction as correct if it matches any one of three annotators. Given low agreement and subjective labels, this metric may inflate apparent accuracy and makes the reported parity with GPT-3.5 harder to interpret.; The abstraction training data is generated automatically by GPT-3.5/GPT-4 rather than by humans, so the learned abstraction behavior may inherit biases or stylistic artifacts from teacher models; this is acknowledged indirectly but not deeply analyzed.; All results are reported from single runs (Appendix F.3), so variance and statistical robustness are unclear, especially for relatively small datasets and close model comparisons.; The HCI study demonstrates positive reception, but it does not rigorously establish that the system actually reduces harmful disclosures or improves posting decisions in real-world longitudinal use.
