Summary: The paper proposes an automated method to characterize NLI datasets into easy/ambiguous/hard subsets using training dynamics. For each test example, the authors train two classifiers directly on the target split—one with premise+hypothesis and one with hypothesis only—and extract 8 features (confidence, variability, correctness, AUM from both settings). A 3-component Gaussian Mixture Model clusters examples into difficulty levels, which are then interpreted as a stronger test set with reduced spurious correlations. Experiments on SNLI, MultiNLI, and FEVER show that the hard split yields much lower accuracy and weaker hypothesis-only performance, and heuristic artifact measures become less label-correlated. The same procedure is also applied to training data, where filtering subsets can sometimes match or approach full-data performance on Naik et al.'s stress test using less data.

Strengths: Addresses an important and timely problem: standard NLI benchmarks contain annotation artifacts and shortcut correlations that inflate performance.; The core idea is simple and practically useful: use training dynamics plus hypothesis-only behavior to identify likely easy/artifactual versus hard examples.; The method is largely model-agnostic in formulation and is tested with both RoBERTa and DeBERTa.; The paper evaluates on multiple datasets (SNLI, MultiNLI, FEVER), which improves breadth beyond a single benchmark.; Results are intuitively consistent: the hard split has substantially lower P+H accuracy and near-chance H-only accuracy on SNLI/MultiNLI, suggesting reduced shortcut exploitation.; The heuristic analysis of spurious correlations and the qualitative examples help interpret what the discovered splits contain.; Applying the same characterization idea to training-set filtering is an interesting secondary contribution with some promising empirical results.; The paper is generally well motivated and clearly connected to prior work on dataset cartography, AUM, and NLI stress testing.

Weaknesses: The central evaluation protocol is problematic: the method characterizes the test set by training on the test set itself. Even if used only to extract dynamics, this makes the notion of a 'test-set difficulty characterization' less clean and weakens claims about realistic evaluation.; The paper sometimes overstates what is shown. Reduced heuristic correlations and lower accuracy on the hard split do not by themselves establish a 'more accurate measure of real-world performance.'; The clustering design appears under-justified: the number of clusters is fixed to 3, and there is no ablation or model-selection evidence that GMM with 3 components is optimal.; The comparison to prior methods is not fully fair because the proposed method uses richer supervision signals (including hypothesis-only dynamics) than Data Maps/AUM baselines.; The empirical validation focuses mostly on internal consistency (harder split => lower accuracy) rather than external validity, e.g., correlation with human judgments of difficulty or transfer to independent challenge sets beyond Naik et al.; The FEVER setup is somewhat awkward because FEVER is repurposed as NLI with asymmetric premise/evidence structure; conclusions about spurious correlations there are less directly comparable.; Some claims of model-agnostic generalization are only weakly supported; cross-model results show similar trends, but not rigorous agreement metrics between partitions.; The paper does not sufficiently discuss computational cost: characterizing a dataset requires multiple training runs on the target split, which may be expensive for large benchmarks.

Methodological Flaws: Training on the test set to derive the test-set characterization is a major methodological concern. Although the authors state this is only for gathering training dynamics, the procedure uses labels from the evaluation split and optimizes directly on those examples, which compromises the purity of the evaluation protocol and may entangle memorization with 'difficulty.'; The interpretation of low trainability as intrinsic difficulty or reduced spurious correlation is not fully justified. Examples can be hard for many reasons, including label noise, ambiguity, domain mismatch, or optimization instability. The method does not disentangle these factors.; The statistical analysis of artifact reduction is limited. The paper reports Mann-Whitney tests over heuristic measures across classes, but this does not directly validate that the hard split is artifact-free or that differences are practically meaningful. Effect sizes are not reported.; The heuristic artifact measures are narrow proxies (word overlap, antonyms, negation, misspellings, length mismatch). Showing reduced differences on these proxies does not establish broad removal of spurious correlations.; The baseline construction for Data Maps and AUM appears somewhat ad hoc (e.g., percentile choices for 'ambiguous' subsets), while the proposed method benefits from additional features and hypothesis-only training dynamics. This weakens comparative claims of superiority.; There is no ablation isolating the contribution of each feature group (P+H dynamics vs H-only dynamics; confidence/variability/correctness/AUM). Without this, it is unclear which components are necessary.; The claim that the method generalizes across models is not rigorously established. A stronger analysis would quantify partition overlap, rank correlation of difficulty scores, or agreement statistics, rather than only reporting accuracies on each other's splits.; The use of average intra-cluster confidence from the P+H model to order clusters may induce a circular definition of difficulty tied to the same model family used for characterization, rather than an independent notion of challenge.
