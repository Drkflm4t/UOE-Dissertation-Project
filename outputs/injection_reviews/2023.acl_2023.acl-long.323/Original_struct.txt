Summary: This paper is a narrative review of literature on zero-shot cross-lingual transfer in multilingual language models (MLLMs). It organizes prior work into five factor categories: linguistic similarity, lexical overlap, model architecture, pre-training settings, and pre-training data. The paper summarizes empirical findings across studies, highlights recurring agreements and contradictions, and proposes explanations for discrepancies, such as differences between synthetic vs. natural language experiments, task-specific effects, and methodological differences in feature-importance estimation. It concludes that cross-lingual transfer is influenced by interacting factors rather than any single determinant, with syntax/word order, lexical overlap, model capacity-sharing tradeoffs, tokenizer/vocabulary choices, and target-language pretraining data all playing context-dependent roles.

Strengths: Timely and useful survey topic: understanding why multilingual models transfer cross-lingually is important for both theory and practice.; Clear organizational structure around five factor categories, which makes the literature easier to navigate.; Good breadth of coverage across linguistic, architectural, and data/training perspectives rather than focusing narrowly on one explanation.; The paper does more than list papers: it explicitly discusses contradictions and attempts to reconcile them.; Helpful synthesis of task-dependent patterns, e.g., differences between token-level and sentence-level tasks, and between low-level vs. high-level tasks.; The tables (especially pages 3 and 13) provide a compact overview of surveyed studies, tasks, models, language types, and investigated factors.; The discussion section identifies concrete future directions, including factor interactions, broader multilingual evaluation, and extension to generative models.; The paper acknowledges limitations such as selection bias, publication bias, and methodological heterogeneity across reviewed studies.

Weaknesses: The review appears largely narrative rather than systematic; paper selection criteria, search strategy, inclusion/exclusion rules, and coverage guarantees are not specified in enough detail.; There is no quantitative meta-analysis or standardized comparison framework, so many conclusions remain qualitative and somewhat impressionistic.; Some reconciliations of contradictory findings are speculative rather than strongly evidenced by direct comparative analysis.; The survey scope is somewhat narrow temporally and model-wise, focusing mainly on encoder-style models such as mBERT/XLM-R, with limited treatment of newer multilingual generative LLMs.; The five-factor taxonomy is useful but not obviously exhaustive; some potentially relevant factors (fine-tuning protocol, evaluation dataset artifacts, alignment objectives beyond pretraining, instruction tuning) are underdeveloped.; At times the paper draws fairly strong conclusions from a small number of studies per subtopic, especially for model architecture and certain pre-training settings.; The review could better separate evidence from synthetic-language studies versus natural-language studies, since external validity differs substantially.; There is limited critical assessment of the quality of the underlying studies; all findings are mostly treated at similar evidential weight.

Soundness Issues: As a review paper, the main soundness question is methodological rigor of the survey process. The lack of a systematic review protocol weakens confidence that the literature coverage is comprehensive and unbiased.; Several conflict-resolution claims rely on plausible hypotheses (e.g., interactions between lexical overlap and word order, or regression-model choice explaining discrepancies) but are not validated through formal re-analysis.; The paper sometimes aggregates findings across heterogeneous tasks, models, and evaluation setups, which may overstate coherence of the evidence base.; No explicit rubric is provided for weighing stronger vs. weaker empirical evidence, making some synthesized conclusions less firmly grounded than they appear.; That said, the paper is generally logically consistent, accurately caveated, and appropriately acknowledges uncertainty and limitations.
