  "summary": "The paper presents a broad empirical study of task-specific knowledge distillation for natural language generation under a medium-resource setting with abundant unlabeled inputs. It compares encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only architectures, encoder vs. decoder pruning, several KD objectives, and a family of pseudo-target (PT) augmentation strategies. The main proposed method, Joint-Teaching, combines word-level KD with PTs generated by both teacher and student, motivated by exposure bias considerations. Experiments span four English-to-English NLG tasks and two teacher-student pairs, with an additional exploratory GPT-4 zero-shot teacher setting. The paper reports that encoder-decoder models are preferable for these tasks, decoder pruning is better than encoder pruning, unlabeled-data PTs help substantially, multiple sampled PTs outperform single beam PTs, and Joint-Teaching yields the best average gap-closing performance.",
  "strengths": [
    "The paper addresses a practically important problem: compressing task-specific NLG systems under realistic constraints with limited labeled data and abundant unlabeled data.",
    "The study is unusually broad, covering architecture choice, pruning, KD objectives, PT generation strategies, and a new Joint-Teaching variant across multiple tasks.",
    "Including unlabeled data in task-specific NLG distillation is a meaningful and practically relevant contribution, and the empirical gains reported are substantial.",
    "The paper evaluates both task quality and deployment-oriented efficiency metrics such as latency and throughput, which strengthens its practical relevance.",
    "The proposed PT extensions are intuitive and well motivated, especially the move from single beam pseudo-targets to multiple sampled pseudo-targets.",
    "The final results suggest strong compression-performance tradeoffs, with distilled students recovering a large fraction of teacher performance while improving inference efficiency.",
    "The paper is generally well organized and clearly communicates the staged experimental design and main findings."

  "weaknesses": [

    "The exposure-bias motivation is suggestive rather than rigorous; the paper does not establish that the observed improvements are specifically due to mitigating exposure bias rather than simply more synthetic training data.",
    "The proposed Joint-Teaching method is only lightly explored: the 50% teacher/student PT mixing ratio appears heuristic, with no ablation over schedules or proportions.",
    "The empirical scope is narrower than the title suggests: all main tasks are English-to-English with relatively short outputs, and only two student-teacher pairs are used in KD stages.",
    "The GPT-4 extreme setup is underdeveloped and yields mixed results, limiting the strength of claims about transferability of the conclusions to large-model teachers.",
    "The paper does not adequately account for training-time computational cost, even though several proposed methods rely on generating many PTs and repeated teacher queries, which is central to practical adoption."
