  "summary": "The paper studies privacy-preserving activation steering for LLM alignment. It proposes PSA, which computes per-layer steering vectors from paired positive/negative demonstrations, clips the per-example activation differences, and adds Gaussian noise to obtain differentially private steering vectors. These vectors are then reused at inference time to steer arbitrary numbers of queries via post-processing, yielding a claimed overall privacy guarantee across selected layers. The paper evaluates PSA on seven alignment-related multiple-choice benchmarks across several open-source LLM families and sizes, compares against zero-shot and non-private steering baselines, reports limited degradation on MMLU and GPT-4 judged open-ended generation, and introduces a canary-based membership inference attack tailored to activation steering.",
  "strengths": [
    "Addresses an underexplored and practically relevant problem: privacy risks in inference-time activation steering rather than training/fine-tuning.",
    "Method is simple, easy to implement, and conceptually well aligned with standard DP mechanisms (clip per-example contributions and add Gaussian noise).",
    "Clear high-level privacy intuition: privatize the released steering vectors once, then answer unlimited downstream queries via post-processing without further privacy cost.",
    "Broad empirical coverage across multiple model families, model sizes, and seven alignment-related behaviors.",
    "Includes both utility evaluation (behavioral accuracy, GPT-4 judged generation quality, MMLU) and empirical privacy auditing via a custom canary-based MIA.",
    "The scaling analysis suggesting larger models tolerate privatized steering better is interesting and potentially useful for practice."
