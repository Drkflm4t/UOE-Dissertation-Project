  "summary": "The paper presents a broad empirical study of task-specific knowledge distillation for natural language generation under a medium-resource setting with additional unlabeled data. It compares encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only architectures, pruning strategies, several KD objectives, and a family of pseudo-target (PT) augmentation methods. The main proposed contribution is to extend sequence-level KD by using multiple sampled PTs on labeled and unlabeled inputs, and a new Joint-Teaching method that applies word-level KD on PTs generated by both teacher and student. Experiments span four English NLG tasks and two teacher-student pairs, with an additional exploratory GPT-4 extreme setup. The reported results suggest that encoder-decoder students are preferable, decoder pruning is better than encoder pruning, unlabeled PTs help substantially, sampled multiple PTs outperform beam PTs, and Joint-Teaching further improves distilled students.",
