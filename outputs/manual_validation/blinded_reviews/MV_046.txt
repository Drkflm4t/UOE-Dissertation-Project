  "summary": "The paper proposes an automated method to characterize NLI datasets into easy/ambiguous/hard subsets using training-dynamics features. For each test example, the authors train models directly on the test split in two settings—premise+hypothesis and hypothesis-only—and extract 8 features (confidence, variability, correctness, AUM from each setting). A Gaussian Mixture Model clusters examples into three difficulty levels, which are then interpreted as increasingly challenging subsets. Experiments on SNLI, MultiNLI, and FEVER show that the hard subsets have much lower model accuracy and reduced heuristic indicators of known annotation artifacts. The same characterization idea is also applied to training data, where filtering to selected subsets can sometimes match or slightly exceed full-data performance on Naik et al.'s stress tests while using less data.",
  "strengths": [
    "Addresses an important and well-motivated problem: standard NLI benchmarks contain annotation artifacts and inflated performance due to spurious correlations.",
    "The proposed approach is simple, intuitive, and largely model-agnostic at the feature level, combining standard training-dynamics statistics with hypothesis-only signals.",
    "Empirical results show clear monotonic degradation from easy to hard splits on SNLI and MultiNLI, suggesting the clustering captures some notion of difficulty.",
    "Including FEVER broadens the scope beyond canonical NLI benchmarks and suggests the method may identify genuinely difficult examples even when hypothesis-only bias is weaker.",
    "The training-set filtering experiments are potentially useful and compare against relevant baselines such as Data Maps and AUM.",
    "The paper includes qualitative discussion and heuristic analyses that help interpret what the discovered splits contain."
  ],
  "weaknesses": [
    "The central methodology trains models on the test set to characterize the test set, which is highly unconventional and undermines the claim that this yields a clean evaluation benchmark.",
    "The paper conflates difficulty, learnability, and spurious-correlation prevalence; lower accuracy on a subset does not by itself establish that the subset is less biased or more realistic.",
    "The statistical evidence for reduced spurious correlations relies on a small set of shallow heuristics, which are incomplete proxies for annotation artifacts and may miss many important biases.",
    "The hard subsets are heavily label-skewed (especially toward neutral), so reduced performance may partly reflect changed class composition rather than genuinely harder reasoning.",
    "The claim of model-agnosticism is only weakly supported by comparison between two closely related transformer encoders; broader architectural diversity is not tested.",
    "The training-set characterization results are evaluated mainly on Naik et al.'s stress tests rather than on broader in-domain and out-of-domain generalization metrics, limiting conclusions about data quality improvements."
  ],

    "The method characterizes the test set by fitting models directly on the test examples for multiple epochs.     "The model-agnostic generalization claim is overstated. Cross-model transfer is tested only between RoBERTa and DeBERTa, both pretrained transformer encoders with similar fine-tuning behavior; this does not establish robustness across substantially different model families or calibration regimes."
