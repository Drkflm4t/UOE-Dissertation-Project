  "summary": "The paper proposes an automated method to characterize NLI datasets into easy/ambiguous/hard subsets using training-dynamics features. For each test example, the authors train models directly on the test split in two settings—premise+hypothesis and hypothesis-only—and extract 8 features (confidence, variability, correctness, AUM in both settings). A Gaussian Mixture Model clusters examples into three difficulty levels, which are then interpreted as a harder, less artifact-prone stress test. Experiments on SNLI, MultiNLI, and FEVER show monotonic drops in accuracy from easy to hard splits and reduced hypothesis-only performance on hard subsets. The same characterization is also applied to training data, where selected subsets reportedly match or exceed full-data performance on Naik et al.'s stress tests while using less data.",
  "strengths": [
    "Addresses an important and well-motivated problem: benchmark contamination by annotation artifacts and spurious correlations in NLI.",
    "The core idea of combining premise+hypothesis and hypothesis-only training dynamics is intuitive and relevant to NLI-specific biases.",
    "Uses multiple datasets (SNLI, MultiNLI, FEVER) and compares across two encoder families (RoBERTa, DeBERTa), which broadens empirical scope.",
    "The hard subsets do appear substantially more challenging, and hypothesis-only accuracy drops toward chance on those subsets, which is consistent with the intended goal.",
    "The training-set characterization experiments are potentially useful, suggesting a practical data-selection application beyond evaluation.",
