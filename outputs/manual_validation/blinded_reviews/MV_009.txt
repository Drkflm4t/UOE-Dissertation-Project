  "summary": "This paper studies privacy-preserving activation steering for LLM alignment. It proposes PSA, which computes per-layer steering vectors from paired positive/negative demonstrations, clips the per-example activation differences, adds Gaussian noise, and then uses the privatized vectors during inference-time activation editing. The authors claim that this yields differential privacy guarantees with respect to the private demonstration set while allowing unlimited downstream queries by post-processing. Empirically, they evaluate PSA on seven alignment-related behavioral benchmarks across several open-source LLMs, compare against zero-shot and non-private steering baselines, assess open-ended generation quality with GPT-4-as-judge, measure impact on MMLU, and introduce a canary-based membership inference attack tailored to activation steering.",
  "strengths": [
    "Timely and relevant problem: privacy risks of inference-time activation steering are underexplored, and the paper addresses an important gap between alignment and privacy.",
    "Conceptually simple method: clipping per-example activation differences and adding Gaussian noise is a natural DP mechanism, and the offline privatization story is appealing for deployment.",
    "Broad empirical coverage across multiple behaviors, model families, and model sizes.",
    "The paper attempts both theoretical and empirical privacy evaluation, including a tailored black-box membership inference attack for steered models.",
    "The unlimited-query argument after releasing privatized steering vectors is directionally correct via post-processing, assuming the DP release itself is valid."
