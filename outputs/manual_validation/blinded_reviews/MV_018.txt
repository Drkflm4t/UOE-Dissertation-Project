Review ID: MV_018
Validation Track: RQ1
============================================================

Summary: The paper proposes an automated method to characterize NLI datasets into easy/ambiguous/hard subsets using training dynamics. For each test example, the authors train two models on the test split itself—one with premise+hypothesis and one hypothesis-only—and extract 8 features (confidence, variability, correctness, AUM from both settings). A 3-component Gaussian Mixture Model clusters examples into difficulty levels, which are then interpreted as a stronger stress test. Experiments on SNLI, MultiNLI, and FEVER show that the hard subsets have much lower accuracy, reduced hypothesis-only performance, and weaker heuristic artifact signals. The same characterization procedure is also applied to training data, where selected subsets can match or slightly improve stress-test performance using less data than full training and outperform Data Maps/AUM baselines in several settings.

Strengths: Addresses an important and timely problem: standard NLI benchmarks contain annotation artifacts and spurious correlations that inflate reported performance.; The core idea is intuitive and practically useful: use training dynamics plus hypothesis-only behavior to automatically identify harder and less artifact-prone examples without manual adversarial construction.; Methodologically, the paper extends dataset cartography in a nontrivial way by combining multiple training-dynamics features and clustering them with a GMM rather than relying on fixed percentile thresholds.; The empirical results are broad, covering SNLI, MultiNLI, and FEVER, and include both test-set characterization and training-set filtering.; The hard splits appear meaningfully more challenging: accuracy drops substantially for both standard and hypothesis-only models, supporting the claim that these subsets reduce shortcut exploitation.; The paper includes analyses of heuristic artifact measures and statistical testing, which strengthens the interpretation that the hard subsets contain fewer spurious correlations.; Cross-model transfer between RoBERTa and DeBERTa is a useful check suggesting that the discovered difficulty structure is not entirely model-specific.; The training-set characterization results are interesting and potentially impactful for data-efficient training, showing competitive performance with substantially reduced data.; The paper is generally well motivated, clearly structured, and supported by qualitative examples and informative figures/tables.

Weaknesses: The central test-set characterization procedure trains directly on the test set to obtain training dynamics. Although the authors state this is only for characterization, this is unconventional and weakens the claim that the resulting split is a clean evaluation benchmark independent of model fitting.; The notion of 'difficulty' is partly operationalized by how easily a specific encoder family can fit examples over 5 epochs, so the resulting split may reflect model-specific learnability rather than intrinsic linguistic difficulty.; The clustering design appears somewhat fixed (e.g., exactly 3 clusters, GMM choice) without sufficient ablation or model-selection evidence showing that this is the best granularity.; The heuristic analysis of spurious correlations is limited to a relatively small set of surface features; reduced significance on these heuristics does not fully establish that the hard split is free of artifacts.; Some claims are stronger than the evidence supports, especially the suggestion that the method provides a 'more accurate measure of real-world performance,' which is difficult to validate directly.; The training-set filtering results are mixed: in several tables the full dataset still gives the best overall score, so the gains from subset selection are not uniformly compelling.; There is limited discussion of variance across random seeds or stability of the discovered clusters, which is important for a method based on training dynamics and mixture modeling.

Methodological Flaws: A major methodological concern is the use of the test set itself for supervised training to derive characterization features. Even if labels are not used for final evaluation on that same fitted model, the benchmark split is produced by directly fitting to test labels, which compromises the usual train/test separation and may bias the benchmark toward the inductive biases of the characterization models.; The claim of model-agnosticism is only partially supported. The paper compares RoBERTa and DeBERTa, but both are closely related transformer encoders fine-tuned for classification. This is insufficient to justify broad model independence.; The statistical argument that the hard split has 'no evidence for spurious correlations' is too strong. Failure to detect significant differences under the chosen heuristics and Mann-Whitney tests does not imply absence of artifacts; it only shows reduced signal under those specific probes.; Equation (2) for variability appears nonstandard/incompletely specified as written: the square-root denominator/formula presentation is ambiguous and may omit the usual averaging details, which should be clarified for reproducibility.; The empirical validity of the training-set characterization claim is incomplete because comparisons are primarily against Data Maps and AUM on one downstream stress test; there is no broader evaluation on standard validation/test performance, calibration, or robustness across seeds/domains.; The interpretation of lower hypothesis-only accuracy as evidence of fewer spurious correlations is plausible but not definitive; harder examples may simply be less learnable overall, including for legitimate reasons unrelated to annotation artifacts.
