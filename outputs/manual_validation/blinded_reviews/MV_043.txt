  "summary": "The paper presents a systematic empirical study of task-specific knowledge distillation for natural language generation under a medium-resource setting with abundant unlabeled data. It compares encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only architectures, encoder vs. decoder pruning, several KD objectives, and a family of pseudo-target (PT) augmentation strategies. The main proposed contribution is an expanded PT training recipe: combining logits KD with sequence-level KD, generating PTs for unlabeled data, using multiple sampled PTs instead of a single beam-search PT, and a new Joint-Teaching method that applies word-level KD on PTs generated by both teacher and student. Experiments on four English NLG tasks show consistent gains from PT augmentation and Joint-Teaching, with substantial compression-speed improvements. An additional exploratory section studies distillation from GPT-4 in a no-labeled-data setup, with mixed results.",
  "strengths": [
    "The paper addresses a practically important setting for NLG compression: task-specific distillation with limited labeled data and abundant unlabeled data, which is underexplored relative to high-resource or NLU-focused KD literature.",
    "The study is broad and systematic, covering architecture choice, pruning strategy, KD objectives, PT generation choices, and a novel Joint-Teaching variant across multiple tasks and two teacher-student pairs.",
    "The empirical findings are practically useful: encoder-decoder models outperform decoder-only models for conditional NLG fine-tuning; decoder pruning is preferable to encoder pruning for latency/quality trade-offs; unlabeled-data PTs and multiple sampled PTs provide strong gains.",
    "The proposed Joint-Teaching idea is intuitive and empirically promising, and the paper provides some supporting analysis for the assumption that the teacher can better continue student prefixes than the student itself.",
    "The paper reports practical deployment metrics beyond parameter count/FLOPs, including latency and throughput, which strengthens the systems relevance.",
    "The final results demonstrate meaningful compression with relatively strong retention of teacher performance, making the work potentially useful for practitioners."
